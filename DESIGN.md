# DESIGN.md — BertieV4 Airline Simulator design rules

> Source of truth: the BertieV4 design system on claude.ai (maintained by Bertie in Cowork). This is the ONLY design system for the game, including the Operations Briefings; ignore any older "Airline Simulator" design notes (IBM Plex, gold for the unknown, paper flight strips). This copy was last updated 31 October 2026 (evening: week 1 Sunday, add each day then Σ SUM). Follow it for every colour, type size, layout, component and illustration. If a request conflicts with this file, follow the request and flag the conflict. A newer dated decision from Bertie in a hand-off always wins over this file, even when it reverses an earlier rule; the reversal and its reason are recorded in the decisions log ("Decisions changed, and why"). Never refuse or stall a change because this file says otherwise. Do not edit this file yourself; updated copies arrive in Bertie's handovers.

BertieV4 is an airline-management simulator for upper-primary pupils (Years 5–6), including neurodivergent learners. It must feel like **credible airline operations software, intelligently simplified** — never children's educational software. The pupil is running a real airline; the maths happens because the airline needs it.

> Do not simplify the airline. Simplify what the pupil has to attend to at one moment.
> Show relationships. Direct attention. Let the pupil make the decision.

## Content fundamentals

- Write in short, plain sentences, second person: "Choose where to fly", "Build your timetable". Instructions say what to do *and* what to press: "Type a name for your airline, then press Next."
- Sentence case for screen titles, panel titles, buttons and questions ("Ready for launch", "Open Week 1 Planner"). Uppercase is only for status eyebrows (`eyebrow` style).
- Never call anything in the game a "lesson". Teaching moments are **Operations Briefings**; software requests are **Operations Requests** or **Improve the Planner**.
- Frame gaps as opportunity, not failure: "245 passengers we couldn't carry", not "245 NO SEAT". "£4,570 below forecast", not a red "−£4,570".
- Lead reviews with interpretation: "Strong week · £128,042 profit · 89% seats filled", then the detail.
- Natural time: "1 h 45 min", not "1.75 h" (unless decimal time is being taught). Money as "£19,200"; ticket prices stay real.
- Hints use the pupil's actual numbers and arrive one at a time: "0.8 means 8 tenths. Try 62 × 8 first." Never a generic list of strategies.
- Every calculation answers "why am I doing this sum?" — it appears where its answer becomes useful.
- No emoji as decoration. Small pictograms are allowed on the Operations Wall map for live intelligence (weather, an event), always with a word.

## Visual foundations

**Character.** Professional, dark, atmospheric, angular, calm, responsive, alive — *electrically alive, but calm*. Deep aviation navy, never black; light always comes from the upper left.

**Colour means something.** Use each accent for one job only:

| Token | Job |
|---|---|
| `cyan` | Current focus, interaction, the selected object |
| `blue` | Main navigation and primary controls |
| `gold` | Money, value, opportunity, a result worth noticing |
| `green` | Success, improvement, healthy state, a confirmed answer |
| `amber` | Warning, attention required |
| `red` | Genuine problems only — never "imperfect" |
| `text-soft` | Supporting information |
| `text-muted` | Lowest priority |

- Grounds: the room is `bg-1` (Operations Wall and briefings: `bg-0`). Panels are gradients: main panel `linear-gradient(155deg, panel-2 0%, panel-1 48%, panel-0 100%)`; supporting panel `linear-gradient(160deg, #092643, #051629)`; active panel `linear-gradient(155deg, panel-active 0%, #092743 58%, #06192d 100%)`. Use the `.bv-panel`, `.bv-panel.support` and `.bv-panel.active` classes rather than re-typing them.
- Text: `text` and `text-soft` read on every ground. `text-muted` only on `bg-0`, `bg-1` or `panel-0` at body sizes. `blue` and `red` as text only on `bg-*`, `panel-0` and `panel-1`.
- No white worksheet cards anywhere. Pale "paper" surfaces are retired from the game.
- Glow is light, not paint: `glow-cyan`, `glow-gold`, `glow-green`, `glow-red` only on the current focus, a changed value, new intelligence, a revealed result or live status. If everything glows, nothing is important.

**Type.** One family, `sans` (Segoe UI Variable, falling back to the system sans). Figures use the same face with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums` — no separate monospace, no slashed zeros. Hierarchy by role:

- `screen-title` once per screen → `task` / `result-figure` for the thing to notice next → `section` for panel titles and options → `body` → `support` in `text-soft`.
- At most about three obvious sizes plus the title on one screen. Bold means "look here": ration it, never whole paragraphs.
- A result is never visually weaker than the choice that produced it. Sidebars never out-shout the main task.
- `eyebrow` labels (DECISION, WORK IT OUT, RESULT, MODEL, NEW INTELLIGENCE, LIVE OPERATIONS, FORECAST, OPPORTUNITY) sit above the content they classify, in its semantic colour.

**Geometry.** Square: `radius-none` for panels, buttons and nav; `radius-field` for answer fields; `radius-chip` for small chips. Panels have a 1px `line` border, `shadow-panel`, and a 1px light edge along the top (`linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(150,205,255,.40), rgba(150,205,255,.08) 58%, transparent)`). No rounded cards, no coloured left-border cards except the focus rail.

**Spacing.** `s1` 4 · `s2` 8 · `s3` 12 · `s4` 18 · `s5` 28. Panel padding `s4`; gaps between panels `s4`; related values sit close — never scatter a comparison across the full width.

**Layout.** Aim for 60% primary task / 30% supporting / 10% reference, with one visual centre of gravity. Only a monitoring wall gets equal-weight panels. Controls stay where they are: a **Fixed Inspector** strip (Earlier · Later · Change route · Remove · Done) instead of boxes that follow the selection. Disclose progressively: early game shows detail; later game shows "6 routes · 18 services · 87% filled · 2 need attention" and expands on demand.

## The attention system

- **Focus rail** — `focus-rail` on the current task: work here now.
- **Local spotlight** — the working region is the active panel gradient; neighbours stay as standard panels. Never darken the whole screen, with one exception: a first-visit **tour** darkens everything except the panel it is describing (header, sidebar and footer included) to about 60% (overlay `rgba(1,6,14,.44)`), like a sheet's focus (decided 21 Oct 2026).
- **First-time control callout** — when a control first appears (Add / change routes for new routes, the Mon–Fri / Sat–Sun switch), it gets a 2px `cyan` ring with a soft glow and one Sheet-Callout-style note (eyebrow NEW · …, a title, one or two plain sentences, **Got it**). One callout at a time; each shows once. The note sits in empty space; if it can't sit next to the control it is joined to it by a thin `cyan` leader line routed through gaps (never through a flight block or text). It never covers figures the pupil needs.
- **Active datum** — the key number gets a cyan underline or gold treatment, not a box.
- **Result plate** — after an action the result becomes the dominant area; the options that led to it recede.
- **Change pulse** — only the values that changed light briefly (`t-data`).
- **Cause → effect** — light values in sequence (fare → passengers → revenue → profit) rather than explaining in a paragraph.
- **Information levels** — background (muted panel) · opportunity (brighter surface, gold or cyan accent) · significant change (amber/red border, NEW INTELLIGENCE eyebrow, wakes once with `t-wake`, then settles).

## Maths in the airline

- **Every sum happens on the airline's spreadsheet** (decided 19 Oct 2026). Sums live inside the business, on its own **Sheet**, never on a worksheet. The Studio and the other planning screens are for playing with the plan only (moving flights, changing fares, watching seats fill), with no pop-up sums; one button, **Work out my plan ▶**, opens the sheet with his choices as columns.
- **One look for every sheet: Airline** (decided 19 Oct 2026). A real spreadsheet (column letters, row numbers, a formula bar, click cells, type in the cell) dressed for the airline (flags in column headers, line icons in row labels, seat-fill bars and FULL chips, a header band with a cyan underline, ƒ on cells the airline works out, NEW on new rows, icon tabs). The look never changes during the game; the sheet grows. See **Sheet**.
- **How a sum is built in year one:** the part being worked lights (2px cyan frame, active gradient) and the rest of the sheet dims to 62%; he clicks the cells the sum uses (cyan outline and an A / B tag; the cell names go into the formula bar, "= F5 − F8", with the words after them, "Total revenue − Total costs"); operators are typed or chosen from × ÷ + −; the answer cell becomes the input (magenta outline, £ and a caret); he works it out in his book, writes Sum n beside it, types the answer in the cell and presses Enter. **The figures stay on the sheet and are never written out as the sum. No tile box.** See **Calculation Builder**.
- When solved: the cell fills (money in gold) and lights `green` once (`t-result`), and whatever it feeds updates and lights too: "my calculation changed the airline".
- Worked examples sit behind "Show me how" / "Give me a hint", and use exactly the structure of the pupil's own sum. No partitioning grid. Nothing forces a worked example on him.
- **The class method lives in the hint** (30 Oct 2026, evening): a paper sum's "Give me a hint" uses the class's own method and words, with Bertie's long multiplication grid where it helps (as in the work booklet): "£90 is 9 × 10. First work out 19 × 9, then multiply by 10." Never "take the 0 off" or "put the 0 back". The Finance watch panel on Launch Day is retired.
- **Formula keys and reference colours** (30 Oct 2026; colours extended 31 Oct): a row of keys sits beside the formula bar, to its right: × ÷ + − (40 × 34) and **Enter ↵** (78 wide, a thicker bottom edge), square, `#0d2346` with a 1px `#2b5c9e` border; a pressed key lights (`#14457a`, `cyan` border, `glow-cyan`); **Σ SUM** joins them once he has been shown it. The formula bar is shortened to make room. As in Excel, each cell or range clicked into a formula gets its own colour, used for its outline, its tag and its name in the formula bar, **seven colours in click order, repeating after seven**: `cyan`, `ref-2` violet `#b48cff`, `ref-3` coral `#ff8f70`, `ref-4` lime `#a9e05a`, `ref-5` pink `#ff9ccf`, `ref-6` teal `#3fd9c2`, `ref-7` sand `#e8c99a`. Sums of three or more cells get coloured outlines without letter tags. Undo stays at the end of the formula bar.
- **The job first** (30 Oct 2026, evening): the first time a sheet opens, nothing is lit and no working starts; a Sheet Callout says the job in words (Launch Day: "Work out the ticket revenue for each flight, at each fare", and what ticket revenue is) with one button (**Watch ▶**). The sheet's title says the job too ("Ticket revenue for each flight, at each fare").
- **Column heads name his flights** (30 Oct 2026, evening): a route column's top line says whose flights it counts (flag + "your 2 flights", or "your flight"), and the main line the route and fare ("Dublin at £110"). The first time a column has fewer passengers than people who want to fly at that fare, a Sheet Callout (eyebrow WHY 27?) explains it before that column's sum, with his own figures and flight times: "27 of the 30 can catch your flights. 30 people a day want to fly to Dublin at £110. 27 of them want to go when your 06:00 or 14:15 leaves. The other 3 want a time you don't fly."
- **Sum number.** Every sum has a number (SUM n, gold) that links the screen, the pupil's book and the printed learning record. It is named in the paper signal ("Write SUM 9 beside it") and shown as a small gold tag on the cell it fills.
- **Build or paper: two signals** (30 Oct 2026). Every question on a sheet has a callout pointing at its answer cell, so he knows at a glance what kind of question it is: **Build it on the sheet** (`cyan` header strip with a grid icon: click the cells, press Enter, the sheet works out the answer; "Nothing to write in your book.") or **Work it out on paper** (`gold` header strip with a pencil icon and a 2px `gold` edge: "Work it out in your book. Write SUM n beside it. Then type your answer in C4 and press Enter."). The signals replace the FORMULA tag and the gold SUM n badge on the card beside the sheet. The line under the sheet stays short ("Type your answer in C4, then press Enter." + Give me a hint). Enter is shown as a key. See **Question Signal**.
- **Work it out page = the sheet** (19 Oct 2026). One sheet stays on screen for all of the day's sums: on Launch Day his options are the columns (Dublin at £80, £90, £100, Paris at £90) and then **Your plan**, with the costs under Your plan like a balance sheet (Total revenue, Other costs, Fuel burned, Total costs, Profit). Each sum lights its part in turn. The one-sum pages, their margins diagram and the separate working panel are retired.
- **The sheet's Fuel line is the fuel burned that day.** On Launch Day it is charged at the launch price, £1.20 a litre ("600 L at £1.20"), worked out by Finance (ƒ) and included in Total costs, so profit doesn't suddenly fall in week 2 (30 Oct 2026; "free on Launch Day" is retired). The week's fuel order is placed on the Fuel desk.
- **Launch Day fares** (30 Oct 2026, evening; replaces the morning's pattern and "One flight, two ways"): the three Dublin columns are the fare below his, his fare and the fare above, and he works them **left to right**. (1) The job first. (2) **Finance shows how to build a formula**: a pointer clicks B2 (cyan outline, tag A), presses the × key, clicks B3 (`ref-2` outline, tag B), presses Enter; the sheet fills B4; a Sheet Callout (HOW A FORMULA WORKS) explains: "= B2 × B3 means passengers × fare. Each cell gets its own colour… Pressing Enter tells the sheet to work it out." (3) **He builds the second** (build signal; the sheet works it out). (4) **The third on paper** (paper signal, SUM 1; the class method in the hint). (5) Only then Finance fills in Paris (ƒ) and Your plan; then **Finance builds the profit** with the pointer (clicks Total revenue, presses −, clicks Total costs, presses Enter; a watch, not counted; 31 Oct 2026), and the line says the day's profit. One counted question on Launch Day.
- **Mastered sums fill themselves** (ƒ). Which sums are his and which are ƒ is set per sum in the workbook's sum list. Each new idea arrives as NEW rows and is the one sum he does; a **Sheet Callout** introduces it in words the first time. **Start weeks come from the workbook** (Bertie, 30 Oct evening: everything after week 1 moves along a week, as built in v6.4; weeks named in these rules may be the original ones: the workbook wins).
- **Plan changes** are worked out on the sheet as three columns: **Now · With your change · Difference** ("Is the change worth it?").
- **Event sums** (Stay with you, Business travellers and others that come from an event, not the plan) keep the "rows of ten" model as the picture of why (the group as person figures in rows of ten, 7 of each 10 lit and the rest dimmed, a bracket "7 in every 10 = 0.7", one caption tying it to the event). The sum itself is built on a small sheet under it, the same way as every other sum.
- **The sheet does the adding** (21 Oct 2026). Some sums are **formula-only**: he builds the formula by clicking cells and the sheet shows the answer at once (money in gold); nothing goes in his book and the sum has no Sum number. In year one these are the Sunday review's **Profit this week** and **Average a day**. Which sums are formula-only is set in the workbook's sum list (how = formula).
- **Review sums** (Sunday review) are built on the week's sheet, with the build signal. **Week 1** (31 Oct 2026, second decision that day): **one sheet panel with one formula bar across the top and two separate spreadsheets inside it**, side by side, one for each way of adding up: on the left **1 · Add each day**, on the right **2 · Use Σ SUM** (the method name, numbered, in each sheet's top-left header cell). Columns narrowed so both fit at 1366 × 768 (label 172px, figures 140px); What happened stays on the left, dimmed. The formula bar belongs to whichever sheet is being worked. (1) **Finance adds Monday to Wednesday** on the left, on made-up profits in the example look, with the pointer: clicks each day's profit with + between them (each cell in its own reference colour), presses Enter. The right side is empty apart from a quiet dashed box: "Next: a quicker way". (2) **The left sheet lengthens into his week** (YOUR WEEK, Monday to Sunday, his own figures; the example's rows and figures give way to his): he clicks each day and presses + between them, then Enter. Seven days use the seven reference colours. (3) His total shows (`green` glow) and the left sheet dims a little; **Finance's example appears on the right** and Finance demonstrates **Σ SUM**: presses Σ SUM, drags Monday to Wednesday (dashed `cyan` range), presses Enter. (4) **The right sheet lengthens into his week:** he presses Σ SUM, drags his Profit column Monday to Sunday, presses Enter. (5) Both totals show the same figure ("The same answer, in one drag"); then **Average a day** on the right sheet: click Profit this week, press ÷, click Days flown, press Enter. All four are built on the sheet, not counted; nothing to write in his book. The build signal runs full width under the panel. **Done:** no Done panel; small `green` callouts at the totals and the average; Plan week 2 ▶ appears only then. **From week 2:** Σ SUM by dragging, no example. **Difference from the plan** stays a paper sum (SUM n) when asked; not in week 1.
- **Example look** (30 Oct 2026): when Finance shows a method with made-up numbers, that sheet gets a 2px dashed `text-soft` frame, faint diagonal stripes and a FINANCE'S EXAMPLE · MADE-UP NUMBERS label; the build signal carries an EXAMPLE · WATCH tag. Since 31 Oct the week 1 example sits beside his own sheet (Review sums).
- **What-if fare sheet** (30 Oct 2026, from week 2): a **What if** tab, one row per route (Fare with − and +, Want to fly, Seats filled with its bar, Ticket revenue, Costs, Profit) and a Both routes row. **Free to try:** every number on a row works itself out (ƒ) as he changes its fare; no sums, nothing in his book. The first visit has one Sheet Callout (NEW · WHAT IF, "Try a fare before you set it"). Fares he has changed are outlined `green`; a panel "What changed, against the fares you have now" lists each route's change and the profit a day before and after, with the difference in `green`, and **↶ Back to my fares**. **Set my fares ▶** keeps them.
- **Flight times on a sheet** (21 Oct 2026; **parked** 30 Oct with the flight-time sums, kept here for when they return): a small sheet, routes as rows, columns Distance · Speed (the aircraft) · Flight time; he builds `= B2 ÷ C2` and types the answer as hours and minutes (an h box and a minutes list in the cell), never as a decimal. The routes the game works out carry ƒ with grey figures, and "ƒ worked out for you" sits under the sheet. The sheet is centred and wide, with the SUM n card in one row under it.
- **Unknown colour.** The unknown in a sum is `magenta` (the "?" and the outline of the cell it fills); `gold` is for money. A callout or panel changes state once and never pulses.

## Event cards

Every event card (Storm, Red Kite, crew sickness and the rest) is one centred column 900px wide: (1) a scene band in the departures-board style, seen through glass (rain, one lightning flash on opening for weather); (2) an eyebrow (amber DISRUPTION for weather, crew and technical trouble; cyan NEW INTELLIGENCE for market news), the title at 30px and one context line; (3) the model's numbers as the hero when the event has them (Red Kite: the profit cells start covered by "See profit" and each row is uncovered in turn; Choose buttons appear only when all have been seen); (4) the choices as board keys; (5) the outcome as ONE ROW: RESULT eyebrow, headline and why line on the left, two figure boxes on the right (money against today's total costs as a gold bar segment; reputation stars with the change). Pressing a choice commits it: no Decide button, no "Change your mind", no "What it costs" side panel, no coloured left borders. The footer then shows "Back to today's flying ▶".

Picking up a flight switches the people to its route and lights the band it lands in `green`. ## Motion

Motion communicates state, selection, cause and effect, physical process, consequence or new information — nothing else. Timings: `t-hover`, `t-page`, `t-data`, `t-wake`, `t-result`, `t-fuel`, `t-ambient`. Fuel is physical: cash flows into the tank, the tank rises; later the tank falls and the value used flows into flight cost. Never pulse every panel, flash borders, spin icons, scatter particles or make the pupil wait. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` (or the teacher's switch) turn off all ambient drift, breathing and route motion; meaning stays in colour, border, words and the focus rail.

Screen energy: calculation very calm · planning moderately responsive · fuel physical · Intelligence Wall occasional meaningful change · Live Operations most dynamic · Operations Briefing cinematic but controlled.

## The three places

- **The Desk** — *what am I doing?* Operating plan, fares, timetable, aircraft, fuel, calculations. Moderate energy.
- **Intelligence Wall** — *what do I know?* Darker (`bg-0`), deep navy panels (`wall-panel-2` → `wall-panel-1` → `wall-panel-0`, stat tiles `wall-tile`, map `wall-ocean` / `wall-land`; decided 18 Oct 2026, replacing the old pale royal blue), map-led, 70% scanning / 30% reading. Home hub gold with a halo; active routes cyan; possible routes muted dashed; opportunity gold; disruption amber/red.
- **Live Operations** — *what is happening now?* Moving aircraft, departures, delays. The most motion, same system.

## Operations Briefings illustration

The eleven Operations Briefings are short animated scenes drawn as **stylised operational illustration**: a night-time airport rendered as clean vector shapes, semi-realistic and simplified, never cartoon and never flat icons. The kit (three style frames and 37 scene pieces) lives in the "Operations Briefings Style Frames" canvas; every scene matches it.

- **Ground and light.** The scene sits on `bg-0`/`bg-1`, darker towards the top of the sky. Light comes from the upper left: object gradients run light top-left to dark bottom-right, lit top surfaces get a thin pale edge, and a soft cyan pool (10–13 %) sits on the floor near the light.
- **Depth (2.5D).** Boxes show a lit top face and a darker right face; cylinders a lit top ellipse. Every grounded object has its own soft shadow ellipse, a separate shape so a plane can lift away from it.
- **Colour roles are the game's.** `cyan` for what is current or interactive (a slider, the now-line, the current month, fuel filling); `gold` for money and value (coins, ticket money, cash); `green` a good outcome (19/19); `amber` a half-full flight; `red` only something genuinely wrong. Light (a glow or bright edge) goes on one thing per scene. Passengers who miss out are dimmed to 45 %, never red. Red Kite Air has its own identity (see below).
- **People.** Groups of five, no faces, heads `people-head`; clothing `people-neutral` by default, `cyan` for business travellers and `people-family` for families, only when the scene is about the mix.
- **Aircraft** are side views at one scale (12 px = 1 m), facing left, plain `livery-body` white with a blank tail the pupil paints. Flight strips are the timetable's blue blocks, never paper.
- **Seat plans.** When seats matter, a plane is also drawn from above as a seat plan: nose left, the 19-seat Twin Otter as six rows of 1 + 2 and one seat at the back (a simplified layout). Filled seats are `people-neutral` with a `people-head` circle; empty seats are dark with a thin outline; an empty seat that matters is dashed `cyan`. A full plane is labelled "19/19 ✓" in `green`.
- **What a fare does, beat 4** (30 Oct 2026, evening): one panel with the slider's fare (gold, large) and his two planes as seat plans; as the slider moves, the seats fill and empty, the seat counts change, and the words under the planes change ("42 left behind", "2 seats fly empty"). The ends of the slider show the low and high fares.
- **Money is never a liquid** (20 Oct 2026). Cash is a bank: a vault, coins and a balance. Only fuel is drawn as a liquid.
- **Type and corners** follow the interface: `sans` with tabular figures, square panels, boards, cards and tags.
- **Flat shapes only:** paths, rects, ellipses, linear and radial gradients, opacity. No filters, so every piece exports as plain SVG. Propeller blur is a translucent ellipse; glows are radial gradients.
- **Motion** only for the change being explained: slide, fill, count up, dim, light once. No bounce or loops; reduced motion jumps to each end state.

## Red Kite Air, the rival

Red Kite is a low-fare airline flying from Luton, designed to look like a real, confident brand: something pupils recognise instantly and want to beat when they design their own livery.

- **Colours:** `red-kite` (a deep oxblood, #7A1428), `red-kite-ember` for light on the red and a pinstripe, `red-kite-ivory` for the kite and the name. None of these is on the pupil's livery menu; the pupils' Red (#D7263D) is brighter and stays theirs.
- **Kite mark:** a red kite in flight seen from below, angular, with its forked tail; it always flies up and to the left, tilted 10°. Ivory on kite red, or kite red on white.
- **Livery:** white body; kite-red tail with the ivory kite; a kite-red sweep rising from under the wing to the tail with an ember pinstripe above it; kite-red engines and tailplane; "RED KITE" in tracked capitals, kite red, behind the wing.
- **On screens:** the badge (ivory kite on a kite-red square, square corners) sits beside its fare, on its gate sign, map pins and news. Never red text, never a red fill; red still means a real problem.

## Quality test

Can a pupil tell where to look first within two seconds? Is there one obvious current task? Do supporting panels stay supporting? Is opportunity distinguished from a problem? Does it look like airline software rather than a worksheet? Would it still make sense with animation off? If things fight for attention, don't enlarge the winner — adjust contrast, spacing, local light, position, disclosure or motion.

## Components

Each component's rules. Example figures are illustrative; real values always come from the workbook.

### AircraftCard
One aircraft at a glance: picture, name, the job it does, three specs and its costs.

- Supply: the side view from `assets/Aircraft/` (in the pupil's livery when owned), name, tier, a one-line purpose, seats, speed (km/h), range (km), cost to buy, and cost a day to run.
- Eyebrow says its status: `green` "In your fleet", `cyan` "For sale", `text-soft` "Locked" with the chapter it opens in.
- Price to buy in `gold` at `task` size; running cost in `text`. Owned cards drop the price and get a green-tinted border.
- Keep the specs to three. Deeper detail goes in an Aircraft Comparison, which lines the same rows up in columns so the eye compares vertically.
- The figures shown are from the prototype file's aircraft list. The workbook is the source for real values.

### AircraftComparison
Two to four aircraft side by side, with the same rows in the same order, so the eye compares down each row.

- Supply: the aircraft (picture from `assets/Aircraft/`, name, status), and only the rows that matter to this decision: usually seats, speed and the flight time on the route in question, plus cost when buying.
- Columns are narrow and close together; never spread related figures across the full width.
- The aircraft being considered gets the cyan column; the best value in a row may be `gold`. Owned aircraft are marked with a `green` "You own" eyebrow.
- Time in natural units ("40 min", "1 h 45 min").
- Example figures: Twin Otter and Saab 340 from the prototype's aircraft list; E175 from the BertieV4 spec; flight times worked out from speed. The workbook is the source for real values.

### Button
Square buttons with a tiny physical response: a 1px rise on hover, settling back on press.

- **Secondary** (`.bv-btn`) for Back, Edit and other non-committal actions.
- **Primary** (`.bv-btn.primary`): blue gradient `action-top` → `action-bottom` with an `action-edge` border; the main next step on a screen. One per region.
- **Confirm** (`.bv-btn.go`): green gradient `go-top` → `go-bottom`, label in `on-go`. Only for committing a plan: run, fly, start operations.
- **Small** (`.bv-btn.small`) for in-row actions such as Edit.
- Labels are sentence case, 18px semibold; `▶` may follow a forward action.
- Don't make every button large; don't use green for ordinary navigation.
- The game currently writes white labels on the green button (low contrast); `on-go` fixes that.

### CalculationBuilder
How the pupil builds a sum in year one: **by clicking the sheet's own cells**, as in a real spreadsheet, then working it out in his book and typing the answer into the cell (decided 19 Oct 2026; this replaces the four-tile builder and the separate working panel). Use it for relationships ("which numbers, which operation?"); the arithmetic happens in the pupil's book.

1. **Build.** The part being worked lights (Sheet focus) and the answer cell shows a `magenta` "?". The formula bar's name box shows the answer cell ("B4") and the formula starts "= ". Clicking a cell adds its name to the formula ("= B2"), outlines the cell and tags it A (then B), in the order he clicks, each in its own colour (seven colours in click order, starting `cyan`, `ref-2`, `ref-3`…, with the same colours on the names in the formula bar; 31 Oct 2026) (a sum of three or more cells, such as a week's total, gets the outlines without tags); the words after the formula build at the same time ("Passengers ×"). Operators are typed or chosen from the key row beside the formula bar (× ÷ + − and Enter ↵), with Undo at the end of the bar. **The first formula of the game is demonstrated** (Launch Day): a pointer clicks the cells and keys, presses Enter, and a Sheet Callout (HOW A FORMULA WORKS) explains. A cell or operator that doesn't fit gets an `amber` question about its meaning in the line under the grid ("£90 is the fare in column C. This sum is for Dublin at £80: which column is that?"), never "wrong" or red.
2. **Work it out and type it.** When the formula is complete, the answer cell becomes the input (`magenta` outline, a gold "£" when the answer is money, caret); the gold **Work it out on paper** signal points at it ("Work it out in your book. Write SUM n beside it. Then type your answer in B4 and press Enter.", see **Question Signal**) and the line under the grid stays short ("Type your answer in B4, then press Enter.") He types into the cell and presses Enter. The screen never asks for working.
3. **Done.** The cell fills (money in `gold`) and lights `green` once (`t-result`); anything it feeds updates; the line says "✓ Ticket revenue at £80: £3,040 · Sum 5 in your record". A small **Next sum ▸** sits in the done line and the focus moves on by itself after a moment; the footer's right-hand button only leaves the page and lights after the last sum (on Launch Day, **Ready to fly ▶**) (31 Oct 2026).

- **Wrong answer:** the cell's outline turns `amber` and the line under the grid gives a number-specific hint ("Check your working in the book: 38 lots of £80."); never a red cross. Two tries, then the figure is shown (as the Calculation Instrument). "Give me a hint" sits at the end of the line; "Show me how" is a quieter link.
- **The figures stay on the sheet.** They are never copied out as a sum ("£6,660 − £3,610"): the formula uses cell names, the words say the rule, and the A / B tags link them. No tile box.
- **Sum number.** SUM n links the screen, the pupil's book and the printed learning record: it is named in the paper signal and shown as a small gold tag on the cell it fills.
- **Formula-only sums** (the week's total, the average) use the cyan **Build it on the sheet** signal instead: he builds, presses Enter, and the sheet shows the answer; nothing in his book.
- **The first time** a kind of sum appears, a **Sheet Callout** introduces it in words before he builds it.
- Operations Briefings are different: they are demonstrations, so the game shows every sum and the pupil types nothing.

Example figures (Ticket revenue = 38 × £80 = £3,040, Sum 5; Profit = Total revenue − Total costs = £3,050, Sum 9) come from the game's Launch Day; real values always come from the workbook.

### CalculationInstrument
A sum inside the business: the facts, the calculation with its unknown, and a recessed answer field, all on a dark instrument surface. It replaces every white worksheet card.

- **In year one every sum is built on the Sheet** (19 Oct 2026; see Sheet and Calculation Builder). The sheet's answer cell does this instrument's job: asking (magenta outline, cyan caret), confirming once, the amber query with a number-specific hint, and two tries before the figure is shown. Don't build a separate instrument panel for a sum.

- Supply: an eyebrow naming the business context, two or three facts, the expression, the unit.
- Asking: `.bv-panel.active` (focus rail), the unknown in `.bv-unknown` (`magenta` with a soft glow, may breathe slowly), the field with a cyan caret.
- Confirmed: the `?` becomes the answer in `green` (lights once, `t-result`), the field turns `.ok`, and the airline value it feeds updates and lights too.
- A wrong answer turns the field `.query` (amber) with a number-specific hint, never a red cross. Two tries, then the figure is shown.
- Pupils work on paper; the instrument takes only the answer.
- Very calm: nothing else on screen moves while it is asking.

### CollapsedRouteSummary
The whole network in one line, for later in the game when there are too many routes to show at once.

- Supply: route count, services, overall seat fill, the number of routes needing attention, and per route: services, fill and a one-phrase reading.
- Collapsed: "6 routes · 18 services · 87% filled · 2 opportunities", with the attention count in `gold` (or `amber` for problems).
- Opened: one row per route, those needing attention first with a `gold` rail. Columns sit close together.
- Early in the game show routes individually; switch to this summary as the network grows (progressive disclosure).
- The summary line comes from the BertieV4 spec; the route rows are illustrative.

### CompactKPI
A small figure with its label and, optionally, a trend: secondary context, not a giant dashboard card.

- Supply: value, label in plain words ("Seats filled"), optional Trend Indicator.
- Size reflects importance: at most one `.lead` figure (money in `gold`, `result-figure` size); the rest at `task` weight in `text`.

- When a value changes it lights once (`t-data`).

### DecisionCard
A decision laid out in the order a pupil should meet it: why it exists, what they need to know, one useful calculation, what it means, then the choice.

- Supply: a question title (`screen-title`), a "why" sentence naming the real problem, the two or three facts that matter, at most one calculation (built and answered on the Sheet, 19 Oct 2026: the card opens the sheet with the sum lit and then shows its result, with a link back to the row), the consequence, and two or three choices.
- Steps carry numbered eyebrows so the attention order is explicit; the calculation step's eyebrow is `magenta`, the consequence `gold`.
- Payback is shown as blocks (one per operating day) lighting in `gold`, not as a formula.
- Choices sit together at the foot: one `go` button for committing, plain buttons for Wait or Compare. Never a single right answer: several plans should be defensible.
- The pupil should never ask "why am I doing this sum?"
- Example figures (E175, 80 × £240, about eight days) come from the BertieV4 spec. The game's figures come from the workbook.

### DemandStrip
The shape of demand across the day, so the pupil sees that 98 passengers are not one interchangeable pool.

- Supply: a count per time band (Early, Mid-am, Midday, Afternoon, Evening, or a continuous strip of finer slots), the flexible passengers who will take any time, and the selected flight, if any.
- Bars are standard blue; the bands the selected flight can realistically capture turn `cyan` with `glow-cyan`. Flexible passengers sit apart, hatched in `gold` (an opportunity, not a problem).
- Every bar carries its number and every band its word; colour is never the only signal.
- When a flight moves in the timetable, the highlighted band moves with it (`t-data`).
- **On the full-width timetable** (30 Oct 2026) the strip becomes **Who wants to fly**, lined up with the timetable's hours: each band (Early 06–09, Mid-morning 09–12, Midday 12–15, Afternoon 15–18, Evening 18–22) spans its hours above the lanes, with its word, its hours, its count and one person figure per passenger. Light figures when a flight of the chosen route leaves in that band ("✓ the 06:00 catches them"); dashed figures when none does ("no Dublin flight then": they take the nearest). **Any time** sits at the end in outline figures. A route picker (flag, name, count, All routes) and a three-line key sit on the left. Picking up a flight switches to its route, and the band it lands in lights `green`.
- Example figures come from the BertieV4 spec; the game's come from the workbook.

### FixedInspector
A strip of contextual controls for whatever is selected (a flight, aircraft, route or timetable block), in one fixed place under the workspace.

- Supply: the selection's name line (route · time · registration) and its actions. For a flight (each block's width is its time away, there and back): Earlier, Later, Change aircraft, Remove, Done.
- The selected object highlights where it is (cyan edge with a slow 4s breathing glow); the controls do not follow it.
- Earlier and Later step in quarter hours; the block slides to its new time over `t-data` while the strip stays still.
- Nothing selected: the strip stays, greyed, saying "Select a flight to change it", so the layout never jumps.
- `focus-rail` on the strip marks it as the current working place. Done is the only primary button.

### FocusPanel
The one panel: an illuminated instrument with a 1px `line` border, a light edge along the top and light from the upper left.

- `.bv-panel` — standard panel (gradient `panel-2` → `panel-1` → `panel-0`, `shadow-panel`).
- `.bv-panel.support` — deeper, for supporting and reference information.
- `.bv-panel.active` — the region the pupil is working in now: `panel-active` gradient plus the `focus-rail`. Only one per screen.
- Title row `.bv-phead` in the `section` style, with an optional status eyebrow on the right.
- The consumer supplies the content; padding is `s4`, corners `radius-none`.
- Don't nest panels more than one deep; don't give six panels equal weight on a decision screen.

### FuelPriceInstrument
Today's fuel price with a seven-day line, so the pupil can decide whether to buy now or wait.

- Supply: today's price a litre, the last seven daily prices, and a one-sentence reading in plain words.
- Price in `gold` (it's money); the line in `cyan`, today's point marked. Rising prices take an `amber` trend (a cost going up is a warning); falling prices `green`.
- Pairs with the Fuel Tank: fuel already bought keeps the price paid.
- Axis labels in `text-soft`; no more than two or three gridlines.
- The prices in the preview are illustrative (ending at the £1.50 used in the Fuel Tank example).

### FuelTank
Fuel shown as something stored and used. In words (decided 19 Oct 2026): **"Fuel you buy waits in your tank. Each day pays for the fuel it burns."** (This replaces "buying it moves cash into the tank".)

- Supply: cash balance, litres in the tank, tank capacity, today's price a litre, and (after flying) the value of fuel burned that day.
- Buying: the cash figure falls, a gold mote flows along the line, and the tank level rises over `t-fuel` with a cyan surface and a light top edge. Profit is unchanged, and the panel says so in words: "Fuel you buy waits in your tank."
- Flying (the same instrument later): the level falls, and the value burned that day becomes that day's **Fuel burned** cost, which lights once. A sheet's Fuel line is always the fuel burned that day (on Launch Day charged at £1.20 a litre, 30 Oct 2026).
- The tank is a plain physical shape (square, ticks at quarters), never cartoonish. Under reduced motion the level changes at once; the words still carry the meaning.
- **With this order** (Fuel desk, 30 Oct 2026): while he chooses blocks, the tank shows the level he would have with the order (a striped fill under a `cyan` WITH THIS ORDER tag on Your position); the real level changes only when he presses Continue.
- Example figures here (7,000 L at £1.50) come from the BertieV4 spec. The game's figures come from the workbook.

### GiveMeAHint
Help that arrives one step at a time and always uses the numbers in the pupil's own sum.

- Supply: two or three hints written for this exact calculation, ending with an estimate check ("expect an answer near 50").
- The button sits under the Calculation Instrument (on the Sheet: a small link at the end of the line under the grid); each press adds one hint (sliding in over `t-data`) and counts down what's left. Hints never replace the pupil's working.
- The estimate check is marked `gold`; step hints are `cyan`.
- Make the button more prominent only after a first wrong answer, or when a genuinely new method appears.
- Never show a generic list (estimate, partition, formal method), and never teach a harder method than the sum needs. No grid method.
- "Show me how" (a full worked example in the same structure as the pupil's sum) is a separate, quieter link.
- Example hints are from the BertieV4 spec.

### HeaderStat

### InvestmentBusinessCase

Right: the capacity problem, routes it could serve (as small opportunity chips), the one key calculation (built and answered on the Sheet, 19 Oct 2026: "Check one key number" opens the sheet with the sum lit and the case then shows its result), and a Payback Indicator.

### NavItem
A sidebar row: line icon, label and a one-line description, 70px tall, in a stable left column 220px wide.

### NewIntelligenceAlert
News from outside the airline, shown at one of three levels so the pupil can tell routine from worth-a-look from something-has-changed.

- **Level 1, background** (`.l1`): dark, muted, one line in `text-soft`. Routine information.
- **Level 2, opportunity** (`.l2`): a slightly brighter surface with a `gold` edge and an OPPORTUNITY eyebrow.
- **Level 3, significant change** (`.l3`): an `amber` edge (`red` only for genuine disruption), a NEW INTELLIGENCE eyebrow, and it wakes once (`t-wake`), then settles. It never pulses again.
- Supply: a short headline (what and where), one sentence on what it means for the airline, and the level.
- One level-3 alert at a time. On the Operations Wall the same item can also sit on the map, with a word beside any pictogram.
- On Home, summarise the outside world in a few lines and link to the Wall rather than repeating it.
- The example news items are illustrative.

### OperationsBriefing
A short, full-screen piece of teaching inside the game: one airline idea shown with animation, driven step by step by the pupil. It is never called a lesson. A briefing appears when he is about to need a new idea to make a real decision.

- Three steps, shown in the stepper: **Show** (animate the thing happening), **Reveal** (show why), **Use** (put it next to his own plan, then hand over to the real decision). A prediction is the last moment of Show.
- **Predictions** (20 Oct 2026; replaces the 19 Oct "no guesses" rule). A scene can ask a question; he taps a prediction: two or three answers drawn in the picture as chips, plus **Not sure**, which always counts as an answer. Next appears once he has tapped. Nothing is ever marked right or wrong: no ticks, crosses, red, buzzers or "Correct!". Every question can be answered by looking at what is on screen; if it can't, don't ask it. The very next scene shows what really happens, with his prediction kept small beside it ("You predicted: stays the same"), in the same neutral style whatever he chose.
- **The words sit inside the picture** (20 Oct 2026). The caption sits in the scene next to what it describes, centred, one or two short sentences at 20–22px, on a soft dark plate when the art behind it is busy. The art moves down a little to make room. The bottom bar holds only Back, Next and Pause & discuss.
- **He does things.** Wherever he can drive a scene (drag the fare, press Buy, press Fly 1 hour, drag a flight), he drives it. Watching is never the only thing he does.
- Sums in a briefing are demonstrations: the picture builds the idea first, then the sum appears underneath; the pupil types nothing (no SUM n).
- **No "End of briefing" panel.** The last scene turns into the real screen it was for, and the game takes over.
- **Money is never a liquid.** Cash is a bank: a vault, coins and a balance. Only fuel is drawn as a liquid.
- Supply: a title, the data for the picture, the scenes with their captions, any prediction with its answers, the Reveal (one sentence), and the decision it leads to.
- The normal interface recedes: `bg-0` room, no sidebar. Back is always allowed.
- The reveal recolours what is already on screen (here, business travellers in `blue`, leisure in `gold`) rather than adding new panels.
- Cinematic but controlled. Under reduced motion every animation jumps to its end state and the steps still work.
- A briefing can end in an **Operations Request** ("What would you want the planner to let you change?"): a decision about his planner, and it stays.
- The weekly numbers in this preview are an illustrative shape, not game data.

### OperationsFeed

### OperationsRequest
The pupil notices a limit in the airline's own software and asks for the improvement, so new planner tools arrive because the pupil needed them, not as "feature unlocked" rewards.

- Supply: the limitation in one sentence, the evidence (often the Operations Briefing that came before), two or three possible requests (one fits), and what the planner gains.
- The fitting choice turns `green` and says exactly what changes and when ("From next week your planner has a Days setting"). Other choices get an `amber` question pointing back at the evidence, never "wrong".
- A quiet path at the foot shows where the planner has been and is going (before · requested · later · ahead), so the airline visibly grows in sophistication. No level tracks, badges or confetti.
- Progression in the spec: weekday/weekend timetables → one-day changes (a rugby Saturday) → summer and winter timetables.
- Days setting values (All week / Mon–Fri / Sat–Sun) follow Design Register D66–D74.

### PassengerMix

### PaybackIndicator
How long a purchase takes to earn back its price, shown as one block per operating day.

- Supply: the number of days to pay back (rounded, "about 8") and, in play, the days operated so far.

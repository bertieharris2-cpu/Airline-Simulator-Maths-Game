## What was built

**The week loop (D52).** Chapter 1 is played a week at a time, driven by the Calendar's `weekPhase`. Monday is one planning page (the timetable with the demand curve, the fare chips, snacks, the week's fuel order, the model's *Try an idea*) and then the workings page for the day's hand sums. Tuesday to Saturday run on the operations board one day per step, pausing only for an event card (Red Kite on Thursday, the storm on Friday) or a sum the rules ask for (the Red Kite tenths, the weekend tenths). Sunday flies, then the review: the seven-row table, the week total and projected − actual by hand, the missed-profit line and the use-it-or-lose-it lines. Each day's result is the standing plan on that day plus any event; there is no market noise. From day 29 the week is skipped as a unit, from 2 December the month. `teacherDayByDay` (teacher panel: *Chapter 1 a day at a time*) restores the day flow.

**Launch Day's sums (D54),** in order on one workings page: tickets at the three fares the pupil is weighing (then the fare is chosen), profit for that fare, snack sales at £3 and £5 (then the snack choice), the guided fuel example and his own fuel bill at Wednesday's £1.30. Later Mondays ask what HandSumRules call for: week 2 rent-or-buy and the new plane's revenue (a full plane), week 3 the new route's tickets and its flight time, week 4 the ATR's rent-or-buy, Madrid's tickets, the range check, the flight time and the fuel bill (the litres changed by more than a quarter).

**Money (D53, D55).** The v4.4 Aircraft day costs and list prices, the Finance rows in days, routes and shop dates on the v4.4 schedule, the chapter-1 events on their Mondays. *Buy* is greyed until cash ≥ price + the reserve, with "£X still to save · about N days at your rate" (N from the last seven days' average profit). The missed-profit line is a plan sweep (below). Use-it-or-lose-it from day 15: at each review every open route with no service that week, or under half full, loses a tenth of its passengers to Red Kite (`S.neglect`, applied in `paxFor`); flown properly it recovers a twentieth a week; the review prints "Dublin: 64 → 58. No service flew this week, so Red Kite is taking your passengers."

**The Dragon Air look.** The opening restyled to the five mock-ups Bertie sent: one mid-blue page, lighter blue rounded panels, white headings, the numbered checklist with green ticks, cash (and reputation once it opens) as tiles in the top bar, wider nav tiles with the pupil's own aircraft underneath, a bright blue primary and a green *go* button, striped tables with dark total rows, and **Plan Impact** on the right of the plan page: passengers, seats, empty seats, fuel, costs and the projected profit for the standing plan. The figures the day's hand sums ask for show as *work it out →* until the workings page is complete, so the sums stay sums.

**Old save codes** (the 9 October work) now land in the week: a save on a Tuesday–Sunday lands on the board with the days already flown filled in from its history; a save with no record of sums done by hand (wind dragon airways) gets one planning day with the first-time sums, then the board.

## The engine against the addendum (`balance.test.js`)

The earlier reports' figures are re-based for v4.4: report − the day-cost rise + the second crew the old engine charged on that plan (£250 when the duty day ran over the limit; chapter 1 has no crew now, D51). Every re-based figure holds to the pound; 23 known differences stay noted (timetables the reports spread differently, Istanbul's archetype, Reykjavik's fare).

The addendum's own figures: the mixed day £1,709 (and £1,751 at Monday's £1.20), Paris one service −£341 / −£151 / £39 / £229, Dublin −£549 / −£359 / −£169 / £21, Madrid ATR £5,486, E175 £4,407, E190 £4,388, Twin Otter £73, Geneva −£164 / −£504 / −£1,898, Barcelona Saab £1,554, Amsterdam ATR £843, week-2 Paris morning + evening £930: all exact. Noted, not failures: Frankfurt Twin Otter £140 (report 19 seated, engine 18, as in v3) and Saab £105 (one passenger); the ATR on Dublin (the fare is not stated); and the spread timetables whose departures the report does not give: Dublin £90 3 spread (report £953, engine £1,038 at 07:00/11:20/18:00), the Saab on Dublin 2 spread (£1,275 v £825 at 07:00/16:00), the Saab on Amsterdam (£2,000 v £1,490), the Twin Otter on Amsterdam (£1,242 v £1,490). The Saab figures matter for the week-2 lesson: at the engine's spread the second plane adds about £150 a day over the mixed day, not £580, so renting it barely pays until Amsterdam. If the report's departures were stated the game could be checked against them exactly.

**The sweep.** Every service list of 1–4 open routes per aircraft, two departure patterns (the model's own times, and a spread from 07:00 to the evening; a single service tried morning, midday and evening), the nearest fares either side of each route's base one route at a time, each aircraft in both orders, scored on the week's Wednesday then the best few over all seven days with each day's demand and fuel price, in a clean week without the week's events. About 20 ms with one aircraft and two routes, under a second with three aircraft and seven routes.

## What I could not do, and what I assumed

1. **Monday's fuel.** The plan page orders fuel for the week at the first paid day's price; a tank that runs short on any day, Monday included, is topped up at the day's price plus `fuelTopUp`. The ready step no longer blocks on fuel in week mode.
2. **The rival's fare on the card.** The v4.4 Calendar's `rivalFares` column is empty, so the Red Kite card and the sweep read the event's own `rivalFare` (£50) for the days the event lasts.
3. **Week 1's free fuel.** The Calendar's headlines say fuel is free on Monday and Tuesday; the addendum prices Monday at £1.20. The game follows the Calendar: Monday and Tuesday fly on the launch deal's fuel, the launch sums use Wednesday's £1.30 as the note says.

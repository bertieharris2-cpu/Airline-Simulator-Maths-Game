## What was built

**The week loop (D52).** Chapter 1 is played a week at a time, driven by the Calendar's `weekPhase`. Monday is one planning page (the timetable with the demand curve, the fare chips, snacks, the week's fuel order, the model's *Try an idea*) and then the workings page for the day's hand sums. Tuesday to Saturday run on the operations board one day per step, pausing only for an event card (Red Kite on Thursday, the storm on Friday) or a sum the rules ask for (the Red Kite tenths, the weekend tenths). Sunday flies, then the review: the seven-row table, the week total and projected − actual by hand, the missed-profit line and the use-it-or-lose-it lines. Each day's result is the standing plan on that day plus any event; there is no market noise. From day 29 the week is skipped as a unit, from 2 December the month. `teacherDayByDay` (teacher panel: *Chapter 1 a day at a time*) restores the day flow.

**Launch Day's sums (D54),** in order on one workings page: tickets at the three fares the pupil is weighing (then the fare is chosen), profit for that fare, snack sales at £3 and £5 (then the snack choice), the guided fuel example and his own fuel bill at Wednesday's £1.30. Later Mondays ask what HandSumRules call for: week 2 rent-or-buy and the new plane's revenue (a full plane), week 3 the new route's tickets and its flight time, week 4 the ATR's rent-or-buy, Madrid's tickets, the range check, the flight time and the fuel bill (the litres changed by more than a quarter).

**Money (D53, D55).** The v4.4 Aircraft day costs and list prices, the Finance rows in days, routes and shop dates on the v4.4 schedule, the chapter-1 events on their Mondays. *Buy* is greyed until cash ≥ price + the reserve, with "£X still to save · about N days at your rate" (N from the last seven days' average profit). The missed-profit line is a plan sweep (below). Use-it-or-lose-it from day 15: at each review every open route with no service that week, or under half full, loses a tenth of its passengers to Red Kite (`S.neglect`, applied in `paxFor`); flown properly it recovers a twentieth a week; the review prints "Dublin: 64 → 58. No service flew this week, so Red Kite is taking your passengers."

**The Dragon Air look.** The opening restyled to the five mock-ups Bertie sent: one mid-blue page, lighter blue rounded panels, white headings, the numbered checklist with green ticks, cash (and reputation once it opens) as tiles in the top bar, wider nav tiles with the pupil's own aircraft underneath, a bright blue primary and a green *go* button, striped tables with dark total rows, and **Plan Impact** on the right of the plan page: passengers, seats, empty seats, fuel, costs and the projected profit for the standing plan. The figures the day's hand sums ask for show as *work it out →* until the workings page is complete, so the sums stay sums.

**Old save codes** (the 9 October work) now land in the week: a save on a Tuesday–Sunday lands on the board with the days already flown filled in from its history; a save with no record of sums done by hand (wind dragon airways) gets one planning day with the first-time sums, then the board.

**The sweep.** Every service list of 1–4 open routes per aircraft, two departure patterns (the model's own times, and a spread from 07:00 to the evening; a single service tried morning, midday and evening), the nearest fares either side of each route's base one route at a time, each aircraft in both orders, scored on the week's Wednesday then the best few over all seven days with each day's demand and fuel price, in a clean week without the week's events. About 20 ms with one aircraft and two routes, under a second with three aircraft and seven routes.

1. **Monday's fuel.** The plan page orders fuel for the week at the first paid day's price; a tank that runs short on any day, Monday included, is topped up at the day's price plus `fuelTopUp`. The ready step no longer blocks on fuel in week mode.
2. **The rival's fare on the card.** The v4.4 Calendar's `rivalFares` column is empty, so the Red Kite card and the sweep read the event's own `rivalFare` (£50) for the days the event lasts.
3. **Week 1's free fuel.** The Calendar's headlines say fuel is free on Monday and Tuesday; the addendum prices Monday at £1.20. The game follows the Calendar: Monday and Tuesday fly on the launch deal's fuel, the launch sums use Wednesday's £1.30 as the note says.
4. **The jet fuel sum on Wednesday.** Rule 2 fires the fuel sum on Monday 23 September (the ATR's litres) and again on Wednesday 25 September; the note's "Wednesday: jet fuel by hand" is met by the Wednesday sum, the Monday one is a bonus of the rule as written.

## The UI Widget Design Brief v4.4 (12 October)

Built in `src/opening/parts/p4w_planner.js` with the square-geometry block at the end of `src/opening/parts/p1_head.html`; screenshots in `docs/screenshots/v44/` (`d1-brief` the intro, `d1-plan` the launch planner, `planner-selected` a block selected with its controls, `d1-workings` the Maths Dock beside the plan, `d8-compare` the comparison, `d8-map` the network view, `d8-plan` and `d15-plan` the planner with two aircraft). In the brief's build order:

1. **Square shell and the intro.** Heathrow is the hub, both routes are open from Launch Day, there is no market gate. The aircraft, market, demand and one-service screens are replaced by one intro (the job, the starting position, the five steps, *Open Week 1 Planner*). Every structural panel, button, tab, input and overlay is square; the pupil's decisions and sums sit on pale surfaces, context and results on dark navy.
2. **One connected planner.** The market widgets (city and code, country, flight time, distance, the rival's fare, the fare chips with the people at each fare), the aircraft day, on board, the fuel strip and Plan Impact on one page; the figures the day's sums ask for carry *ƒ work it out*, and a figure that just changed is highlighted for a moment.
3. **The timeline is the schedule state.** A service is a block as wide as its rotation (out, turnaround, back), with the route, the departure and the seats sold; the hatched tail is the turnaround at home before the aircraft is ready again. Press a block for *Earlier · Later · Swap route · Remove*; drag it (a ghost shows the drop, snapped to 10 minutes, red over another block); the arrow keys move it 10 minutes (an hour with Shift), Delete removes it. The service count is the number of blocks; *+ Paris · + Dublin* add one.
4. **Live cause and effect.** Every change re-costs the plan: Plan Impact, the fuel strip and the fare chips' people update at once.
5. **The fuel widget.** The gauge against the tank, the litres by route, the price a litre and the date it starts, the cost (ƒ until the pupil has worked it out), the week's projection (litres burned, cost on the paid days) and Monday's order.
6. **The Maths Dock.** *Cost your plan* or any ƒ opens the dock beside the plan, which stays in view: *Structured working* (the written method with proper place-value alignment, a carry row and working rows, a number pad), *Squared paper*, *Formula* (the model's lines with the last answer masked). A finished figure is written into Plan Impact; the fare and the snack option are chosen once their sums are done, in the dock or on the plan.
7. **Current plan against a test plan.** *Test an idea* opens a test plan the model costs straight away; *Compare plan impacts* shows *Your current plan · Change · Test plan* for passengers, empty seats, turned away, revenue, cabin sales, costs and profit, coloured by what is good for each measure, with a strip naming what changed (*Dublin fare £80 → £90*, *+ one Paris service*, *Paris 09:00 → 10:20*); *Use test plan* and *Reset*.
8. **Network / IWB.** The map is a button away from the planner and comes back with *Today's plan*.
9. **The Week 1 run and review** are the week loop above.

**Where it differs from the brief, and why**

- **Overlaps are never stored.** The brief allows temporary red overlaps with *Approve* disabled. Here the ghost turns red while it is over another block and the drop lands on the nearest free time (a toast says so); a timetable that cannot fit the operating day is refused with a toast. The plan is always a plan the aircraft can fly, so Plan Impact is never costing an impossible day.
- **The fare chips and the on-board tiles are never locked** (12 Oct, after Bertie found the launch market's chips locking and unlocking as services were added). The launch sums weigh three fares around the fare the pupil chose; the dock's pick then confirms or changes it. A new service lands in the first free gap of the day rather than at the end of the list, so *+ Dublin* works between two Paris services.
- **The plan is read-only while the dock is open.** The dock keeps the plan in view, as asked; changing it means *Back*, so the figures being worked out cannot change under the pupil.
- **Monday's fuel order is automatic**: the litres the week needs at the week's price, capped by the tank and the cash reserve, placed when operations start. The pupil costs the fuel and never chooses an amount.
- **The second route's tickets on Launch Day.** The launch sums are tickets at the three fares of the route with most services (the market), then one × for each other route in the plan, then profit, snacks and fuel.
- **No plan rail.** Bertie asked for the stage list down the left to go: it took room the planner needs. The Network map, Test an idea and Back cover what it did.
- **The crew and rotation lesson** (one service start to finish, the three typed times) is not in the week loop; the intro and the help cards (Timing, Demand, Fuel) carry the ideas. Day-at-a-time play (the teacher's setting) still has it.
- **Fit.** Everything on the planner is in view at 1366×768 and 1915×891 with one or two aircraft, a block selected; from week 4, with three aircraft and seven open routes, the planner scrolls a little at 1366×768 (not on a 1080-line board). The dock's sums scroll inside the dock when the written method is tall.

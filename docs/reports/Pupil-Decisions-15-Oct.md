# Two pupils' decisions, chapter 1 week 2 (15 Oct 2026)

This report covers two airlines, gubby airlines and NBN, up to the end of Wed 11 Sep 2030 (day 10 of the game). It comes from their latest save codes.

- **Where the figures come from.** Every "what if" figure was worked out by the game itself on the pupil's own day, with the same fuel price and the same demand.
- **The tables.** They are written so they can be charted, e.g. a line chart of profit by day, or a bar chart comparing the pupil's plan with better ones.

### Decisions to talk through with him
1. **Departure times are the problem, not the fare.**
   - 50 people a day want to fly to Paris, but only 27 fly at his times.
   - Changing the fare barely helps (−£709 to −£590). Changing only the times turns a £668 loss into a £708 profit.
   - The game's reports said this each day: "5 people wanted to fly in the mid-morning… 2 people wanted to fly in the middle of the day…"
2. **He kept a losing plan for 7 days running** and never opened the planner in week 2. At the week 1 review, his plan was already losing every day.
3. **Fewer flights can earn more.** Paris ×2 at good times (+£975) beats Paris ×3 at poor times (−£668). Each flight costs about £1,000 to run, so a flight that's a third full loses money.
4. **A second route.** Dublin had 72 people wanting to fly at £80 and he never flew it. Every plan with Dublin in it was full.
5. **Fuel.** He made no fuel order in week 2, so every flight bought fuel at the pump: £1.57 a litre, 10p over the price. That cost about £36 a day extra.

### Decisions to talk through with him
1. **His fares are too low.**
   - Every Dublin flight has been full since day 1, with 80 people wanting 57 seats.
   - At £90, 62 people still want to fly, enough to fill every seat.
   - That is £20 more on every ticket: about **£1,140 a day** in days 1–3 and **£760 a day** in days 4–7. That comes to roughly **£6,500 over the first week**.
2. **The new plane is parked.**
   - The Saab costs £1,200 a day whether it flies or not.
   - He bought it and pressed *Keep plan* three days running, so the airline went from +£1,200 a day to −£287 a day.
   - Giving the Saab two Dublin flights makes about £1,700 a day.
3. **Paris is under-served.** 44 people a day want Paris at £100, and his one flight takes 19, so 25 are turned away every day.
4. **Spending all his cash at once.**
   - On day 8 he spent £9,000 on the Saab and £4,410 on 3,000 L of fuel, enough for six days.
   - That left too little cash without the loan.
   - Buying fuel early was sensible, because the price kept rising. But 1,000–2,000 L would have left a safety margin.
5. **Good decisions to praise:**
   - Holding his price against Red Kite. Dublin stayed full.
   - Adding Paris at £100 on day 4.
   - Moving the first flight earlier on day 7.

## Notes: what we might change in the game

**Things that would have stopped these losses**

1. **A losing plan can run for days unnoticed.** gubby lost money 7 days in a row, NBN 3.
   - After 2 losing days in a row on the board, the game could stop and send the pupil to the planner.
   - It would show "Your plan lost £X yesterday" and point at the biggest cause: empty seats at bad times, an idle plane, or a low fare.
2. ***Keep plan* is too easy when something is wrong.**
   - NBN's planning page showed "The Saab 340 has no flights yet" and "Projected profit −£287", but the big green *Keep plan* button still went straight through.
   - When a plane is idle or the projection is a loss, *Keep plan* could ask "Are you sure? This plan loses £287 a day", or be disabled until the pupil has opened *Edit plan*.
3. **Full flights are a signal nobody acts on.**
   - When every seat on a route sells and people are turned away for several days, the review could suggest testing a higher fare.
   - NBN ran Dublin at £70 for 10 days with up to 23 people a day turned away.
4. **A teacher alert.** The teacher panel could flag "losing money for N days" or "aircraft idle", so the teacher can step in before the cash runs out.

**Bugs and rough edges to check**

5. **The Red Kite event on gubby's Thursday:**
   - It was about Dublin, a route he doesn't fly.
   - His log records all three choices (Match, Hold and Advertise) on the same day. Check whether the event can be answered more than once, and whether it should appear at all for a route the pupil doesn't fly.
6. **The week 1 projection for gubby read −£3,162 against an actual +£3,125.** This came from the old code being moved to the new version part-way through the week. It made his "difference" sum very odd: a negative minus a positive. Check how the projection is made for a week that started under the old version.
7. **Fuel.**
   - In week 2 gubby never made a fuel order, so every flight bought fuel at the pump, 10p over the price.
   - The old-code route went straight to the board without the fuel desk step. The pump top-up is also only mentioned in the day notes.
8. **Decimal comma.** "10,5" was read as 105. **Fixed today:** a comma before 1 or 2 final digits now counts as the decimal point.
9. **Guessing the operation.** In the Build steps, gubby tried −, ÷, × and + in turn until one worked.
   - After two wrong operations, the game could show a worked example, or ask "Which is bigger?" first.
   - The pupil record already shows the guessing for the teacher.
10. **Loans.** The £5,000 for NBN was added by hand.
    - A teacher-panel "Give a loan" button, with an optional weekly repayment, would let you do this yourself.
    - Repaying it would also give a real reason to watch profit.

## What changed in the game

1. **The turned-away star rule is softer** (Settings `starsTurnedAwayRule`).
   - A route loses half a star (once) when it turns away 1 in 3 or more of the people who wanted to fly, two weeks running.
   - Only weeks from week 5 (Monday 30 September, day 29) count.
   - The game reads "1 in 3" and "day 29" from the setting's words.
   - Where the rule is written out:
     - the stars card on day 15: "once for a route that turns away 1 in 3 or more of the people who want to fly, two weeks running (counted from Mon 30 Sep)";
     - the review's Stars line;
     - the news;
     - the record.
2. **Red Kite's undercut plays on both tracks.**
   - Track B now has Thursday's card on 5 September, its "Stay with you" sum and the Customers have a choice briefing.
   - The teacher panel's note on track B says so.
3. **Track B's concert and Amsterdam fair come from their RouteNews rows.**
   - The words are Fable's: "A stadium concert in Dublin on Saturday 5 October: the city will be full." The good response is "A bigger plane, more flights or the top fare on Saturday only."
   - The game only builds them from the Events rows for a workbook without the rows.
4. **The fault's £800 comes from its EventOptions row.** The hard-coded £800 has gone.
   - The row's sub-line holds both costs ("£800 (before day 29); half a day's profit after"). The card shows only the one for its day:
     - "Engineers in overnight: £800" on track B's day 18;
     - "Engineers in overnight: half a day's profit" on track A's day 53.
5. **Unflown routes are planned on the middle of their range and say "about"** (Settings `unflownProjection`).
   - Until a route has flown a week, the plan works with the middle of the range the pupil sees. That covers seats filled on each flight, the plan's profit and Change network's sort by demand.
   - Example: Frankfurt at £100 shows 40–60 and is planned on 50 (its true figure is 48).
   - The day actually flown always uses the true demand. Real days are marked as they fly (runDayQuick, startFlight, applyResults, applyPeriodResults and simulateDays outside a dry run), in the same way as the daily colour.
   - Where "about" appears:
     - the Studio:
       - the route's flights in the timetable ("about 28/34");
       - the routes line ("about 94% filled");
       - the routes table's seat fill;
       - the plan summary's projected profit;
     - the Changes sheet: the column header of a plan that has such a route ("With your change · about");
     - the Ready page: the profit row and the plan panel.
   - The sheet's cells stay whole numbers, because they are the sums' inputs.
6. **Long haul grows by how it is served too, in year one.**
   - v6.1 withdraws the old rule everywhere. So in chapter 2, a long-haul route now steps each week it is flown: +5% (the sheet's `growthPerWeek` 0.05), up to its ceiling of 1.5.
   - Long haul has no fill bonus, fare drag or decay in the sheet, so a week not flown leaves it where it is.

## Flags and questions

1. **The middle of the range is almost the true figure.**
   - The range is the true figure ± 15%, widened outwards to the nearest 5, so its middle is within about 2 people a day of the truth. For example, Frankfurt at £135 shows 25–40, is planned on 33 and is really 31.
   - If the aim is that the plan should not give the true demand away, the range has to sit off-centre: a hidden lean per route, say. Fable's call.
2. **"Judged from week 5"** is read as: only weeks from week 5 count, so the first −½ can come at the end of week 6 (day 42). The other reading would judge weeks 4 and 5 at the end of week 5.
3. **"1 in 3"** is read as 1 in 3 or more (a third of the demand or more).
4. **The softer rule still bites in the regression.**
   - The test pupil (Twin Otter, Saab, ATR) turns away a third or more on full routes as they grow:
     - track A loses ½ for Madrid (day 56), Paris (day 70) and Amsterdam (day 105);
     - track B loses ½ for Madrid (day 56) and Paris (day 77).
   - The reviews' +½ balance them, so both tracks end chapter 1 on 3★. Under v6.0 track B ended on 2½★.
   - If the rule should rarely bite before a pupil has a bigger plane, Fable could count only routes flown below a fill threshold, or start it later.
5. **Growth after year one** waits for Bertie's "extend" (above).
6. **Long haul's growth columns.**
   - The sheet's `long_haul` row has `growthPerWeek` 0.05 (a fraction, 5%) and a ceiling of 1.5, with no fill bonus, fare drag or decay. The game uses those.
   - The Balance Report's parameters say 4% a week, +2 when full, −2 when not flown, ceiling 1.6.
   - The Settings note names "long_haul_mixed", which isn't an archetype in the sheet.
   - In chapter 2, New York fills 98.5–98.7% under both versions, so Settings' remedy (a 1.4 ceiling) wouldn't change its fill. See Balance.
7. **The undercut's Events rows** still say track A on options 2 and 3, though the first row and EventOptions say both. The game takes an event's track from its first row, so it plays on both. Fable may want to tidy the two rows.
8. **The fault's sub-line** holds both costs. The card shows the one for its day (change 4). If Fable writes one sub-line per period, the game will show it as written.

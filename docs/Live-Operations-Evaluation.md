# Live Operations: what was built, an evaluation of the brief, and where it can go next

*Written after the first pass of the Live Operations / IWB Immersion Brief (Launch Day to Week 3), for discussion.*

## 1. What the pupil now sees

- **The handoff.** "Send to Operations Wall" starts a short ritual on the wall: *Operating plan approved · Dragon Air operations · Schedule received ✓ · Aircraft ready ✓ · Gates opening ✓ · Live operations starting*. With no IWB window, the wall opens full screen on the laptop by itself.
- **A detailed service.** It goes through check-in, preparing, boarding (the seats fill one by one; the seats that will stay empty are dashed outlines), final call, gate closed, pushback, taxi, take-off, the flight with an ETA, landing, the turnaround checklist, the return, and *Ready again*.
  - **The boards** show the gate and the live status.
  - **The aircraft card** shows G-DAAA's flight, passengers, fuel, ETA and next service.
  - **Announcements** come as text, with a soft chime, and are spoken where the computer has a voice.
  - **From Day 1** the flight shows its cabin service: *10 passengers bought snacks · +£30*.
- **How much is detailed.**
  - **Launch Day:** every service in full.
  - **Days 1–4:** the first service in full and the rest briskly. The first Dublin service is always in full.
  - **The weekend after the Regular Operating Plan:** Friday in detail, then Saturday and Sunday compressed.
  - **Each week:** a detailed first day if the plan changed, otherwise one featured flight, then the rest compressed. The calendar ticks over and flights, passengers, tickets, profit so far and on-time count build up.
- **Moments.** All are taken from the model's own figures; none needs an answer.
  - **Milestones:** first departure, first full flight, first Dublin service, the 100th, 500th and 1,000th passenger.
  - **Full service:** "Dublin afternoon service — full · 22 more people could not get a seat", with the waiting people drawn beside the cabin.
  - **Quiet service:** "8 / 19 · 11 empty seats".
  - **Tight turnaround:** "ready at 12:20, leaves at 12:20: no time to spare".
  - **Storm:** a storm over the route in Week 3.

  In a detailed day a moment is a ribbon over the flight. In compressed time the simulation slows down for it, holds, and then says *Simulation resuming*.
- **The end.** The wall shows *Day complete* or *Week complete*: flights operated, passengers carried, full flights, empty seats, on time, projected profit and actual profit, plus *New profit record* when there is one.
- **Pace.** 1× Live, 2×, Next flight (Next event), Pause and Skip to summary are on the wall and on the HQ. The teacher panel sets Brisk, Standard or Slow, sound on or off, and whether the wall opens by itself.

At standard pace:

| Show | Length |
|---|---|
| Launch Day | about 3½ minutes |
| A later day | 2–4 minutes |
| The weekend | about 3½ minutes |
| A week | 2–3 minutes |

## 2. Evaluation of the brief

**What is strong, and worked as written**

- **"Show the consequence first, quantify it afterwards"** fits this pupil exactly. Two examples:
  - The 19 seat dots filling, with 6 people standing beside a full plane, say "capacity" before any sum does.
  - The quiet midday service on Day 3, 8 of 19 seats filled, is the day's *empty seats* calculation made visible.
- **The rhythm of detailed, compressed, focus and resume** works, and it gives the weeks a shape: a featured flight, then the week racing by, slowing for one or two things.
- **Keeping the early game deterministic** was the right call. Every moment comes from the model, so projected = actual still holds to the pound, and the wall never contradicts the maths the pupil has just done.
- **Observe-only moments with neutral wording** ("Quiet service", never "Bad flight") avoid shame and keep slowdowns from meaning trouble. Milestones, full flights and records slow down too.

**Where the brief and the game pull against each other**

1. **Time.**
   - A full service takes about 1¾ minutes at standard pace, so a detailed day takes 2–4 minutes. Across Launch Day to Day 4 that is 12–15 minutes of watching in the first lesson.
   - I kept every service in full only on Launch Day. After that, the first service is in full and the rest briskly.
   - Worth checking in class: is about 1¾ minutes per full service right, or should Standard be a little quicker?
2. **Repetition.**
   - With a fixed plan the same things happen every day: the same full Dublin flight, the same quiet midday one. Shown every week, they would stop being interesting.
   - In compressed time, everyday moments now appear once for each plan, so a week with an unchanged plan is calm (Week 2 has no slowdowns).
   - Real variety will come from the world: events, weather and seasons in the workbook.
3. **A deterministic model can't make something go wrong.**
   - The brief's best example, a timetable too tight so the next flight boards while the aircraft is still taxiing in, needs late arrivals. With exact turnaround times nothing is ever late.
   - The wall shows the tightness ("no time to spare") but no knock-on delay.
   - Real delays should arrive with the uncertainty stage, together with no-shows, as the brief says.
4. **Weather "that you can do nothing about."**
   - The Week 3 storm delays the last flight of the day by 40 minutes on the wall only: no cost, no knock-on.
   - The review says: *Everyone still flew, and the airline did not cause the storm.*
   - A storm that cancels or costs money changes the profit. That is fine once forecasts are allowed to miss, but not while the pupil is checking projected = actual.
5. **The demand surge.**
   - "Dublin demand surge" would need more people than the model has. I showed the real thing instead: the people left behind by a full service, which the model already counts.
   - A true surge (football final, half-term) belongs to the workbook's Events sheet, with the review explaining the difference.
6. **Reputation is switched off,** so the brief's reputation dips and "handled well" effects are not shown. They will matter when reputation returns at the Year 1 review.
7. **One small aircraft on short routes.**
   - The wall is busiest when it has the most to show; with one Twin Otter the boards have 2–3 rows.
   - The map is zoomed onto the network so London–Paris movement is visible. Wait for the second aircraft before judging how "alive" the wall feels.
8. **Sound.**
   - The chime works on any computer. Spoken announcements depend on the computer having a voice installed.
   - Sound is on by default. Turn it off in the teacher panel if it doesn't suit the room.

**Decisions I made that you may want to change**

- Moments never pause for input; *Pause* is always there for the teacher's "What do you think will happen?"
- Full-screen wall on the laptop when no IWB window is open (teacher panel can switch this off).
- The storm is placed on the aircraft's last flight that day, whichever route that is (Dublin, with the scripted plan), so nothing is knocked on.
- Months and the year keep the plain run for now.

## 3. Brainstorm: keeping the immersion as the airline grows

**The months (next stage)**
- **The brief's month timeline, built from the workbook.**
  - The first day is detailed only if something changed; otherwise there is one featured flight a week, and the month races by in day chips.
  - Focus moments come from the **Market and Events sheets**, which already hold the right material:
    - "Storm over the Irish Sea" in week 5;
    - "Fog season begins at London" in week 11, with early departures delayed;
    - "FUEL SPIKE! £3.00 a litre this week";
    - the football final, the first snow in the Alps, half-term.
- **A fuel board on the wall.** The price flips when it changes, and a "fuel spike" moment shows the tank level against the new price. This ties into the fuel-buying idea from yesterday.
- **Month complete:** the best day, the busiest flight, the month's record.

**The second aircraft**
- **Delivery as a moment:** the aircraft appears on the approach, lands, taxis to its stand, and *Welcome to the fleet · Saab 340 · G-DAAB*.
- **The first two-aircraft day in full.** Two aircraft cards, two planes moving independently, a full departures board, *First two-aircraft departure*. One may be ready while the other is still in turnaround.
- **Featured flight on demand.** Tap any flight on the board to follow it; the other aircraft carry on in the background.

**When uncertainty is taught (forecast stops meaning "exact")**
- **No-shows:** "17 booked · 15 boarded · 2 did not travel".
- **Variable turnarounds:** the tight timetable from the brief really does make the next flight late, and the review counts the knock-on.
- **Weather that cancels or delays at a cost.** Then, as decisions:
  - delay or cancel (the brief's decision pause);
  - later: a spare aircraft or rebooking.

  "Handled well" can soften the reputation dip.

**The airline type (from the Year 1 review)**
- **The wall reflects the choice:**
  - **Premium:** priority boarding, lounge, complimentary service cards.
  - **Value:** quick boarding, packed flights, paid snacks and bags (the workbook's reserved ancillary fields).
  - **Full service:** a mix.
- **Announcement style and cabin cards change with it,** so the strategy is visible, not just a number.

**Long haul and jets**
- **Evening boarding, a long arc across the map, time zones and local arrival time.**
- **Day and night shading,** with runway lights for late services.
- **The first jet's faster movement:** the same wall language at a grander scale, as the brief says.

**Records and the pupil's own story**
- **A ladder of operational achievements:** busiest day, best load factor, first full week, 10,000th passenger, every flight on time.
- **A short captain's log the wall keeps,** for example "Week 3: storm over Dublin, everyone still flew". The review can point back to it.

**Making new moments data, not code**
- **Give the workbook's Events sheet the brief's §49–50 fields:**
  - type, trigger date/time, affected route or flight;
  - severity, observe or decide, slow the simulation or not;
  - visual treatment, effects, headline, subtext.
- **Then new moments can be written in Claude chat** and imported, like the routes and aircraft. For now the Week 3 storm sits in the beat data as `"live": {"weather": {"day": 2, "delay": 40}}`; it should move to the workbook.

## 4. Questions for you after testing

1. Is about 1¾ minutes per detailed service the right length, or should Standard be quicker? (The teacher panel's Brisk is about 40% quicker.)
2. Sound on by default, or off?
3. Should a moment ever pause by itself so you can ask "What do you think will happen?", or is the Pause button enough?
4. In the first month, should weather ever cost money or cancel a flight, or only delay it until uncertainty is taught?

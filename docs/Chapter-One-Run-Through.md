# Chapter one: a run-through

*For Bertie, 30 Oct 2026; Launch Day and week 1 brought up to date with hand-off 3 the same evening; everything after week 1 moved a week with hand-off 1c and workbook v6.4 (31 Oct). What a pupil meets from Launch Day to the chapter-one review in the current build (workbook v6.2 and the 30 Oct hand-offs, then v6.3 and hand-off 3): the pauses, the maths at each one, and the questions he could be asked. Figures marked "e.g." come from a test run (the toolkit's TEST Air); his own will differ.*

## The shape of the chapter

- **Dates.** Launch Day is **Mon 2 Sep 2030 (day 1)**. The **chapter-one review is Sat 28 Dec 2030 (day 118)**, and the test build stops there with one card.
- **How time runs.**
  - Week 1: Launch Day, then a quick check of the plan, and Tuesday to Sunday fly on their own (hand-off 3).
  - Weeks 2 to 4: a week at a time, with a **Sunday review**, a **Monday plan and plan check**, and **"Week N is flying"** (hand-off 1c).
  - Week 5 (day 29): the Sunday review, the Monday plan and the board.
  - From week 6 (day 36): a week at a time, with the review opening the Monday.
  - From December: a month at a time.
- **A pause** is the review and the next plan together. At each pause he is asked **at most two counted questions**: a figure he works out in his book and types in. **His own plan change's sums come first**; Finance's scheduled questions fill what is left, on Monday, after the plan is settled (hand-off 1c, countingPriority). Anything else on the screen is worked out by Finance (ƒ), or he builds it as a formula on the sheet, which doesn't count. Nothing is asked between pauses.
- **Every question shows a Question Signal:**
  - **cyan "Build it on the sheet"**: click the cells and press Enter; doesn't count;
  - **gold "Work it out on paper"**: work it in his book, write the Sum number beside it, type the answer and press Enter; counts.
- **Parked for this build:** all one-off events and news, the Red Kite undercut, snacks and cabin sales, flight-time sums and the Flight times page, and the briefings Customers have a choice, One big Saturday, How long will it take? and Snacks on board.

## Week by week

### Launch Day: Mon 2 Sep (day 1)

**What happens.**
- A TEST airline skips the naming and painting; a pupil's own airline names it, paints the Twin Otter and takes the launch deal.
- The Studio opens on the **full-width timetable** with Dublin and Paris in the Add a flight tray (no Choose where to fly page). He drags flights onto the plane and sets the fares.
- Then **Work out my plan ▶** opens the **Launch Day sheet**, "Ticket revenue for each flight, at each fare". The column heads say whose flights they count: "your 2 flights" over "Dublin at £80".

**Briefings.**
- *What a fare does*: the first time he sets a fare. Beat 4 is one panel: the planes fill and empty as he moves the slider from £70 to £140.
- *How an airline makes money*: in the plan, but **not built yet**.

**Concepts.**
- Ticket revenue = passengers × fare, and a formula that does it: = B2 × B3.
- A fuller plane does not always earn more.
- Profit = revenue − costs.
- Fuel is a cost: 600 L at £1.20 = £720, in Total costs.

**The sheet's order (hand-off 3): left to right, the job first.** With his fare at £80 the three Dublin columns are £70, £80 and £90. If his fare is the bottom of the route's table (£70 on Dublin), the columns are his fare (demonstrated), the fare above (built) and the next one above (on paper): £70, £80, £90 (Settings launchDayColumns, hand-off 1c).
1. **The job first:** "Work out the ticket revenue for each flight, at each fare", and **Watch ▶**.
2. **Finance builds B4:** a pointer clicks B2, presses ×, clicks B3 and presses Enter (e.g. 38 × £70 = £2,660).
3. **HOW A FORMULA WORKS**: "= B2 × B3 means passengers × fare". B2 is cyan and B3 violet. Then **Your turn ▶**.
4. **He builds C4** with the keys or the keyboard and presses Enter himself (e.g. £3,040). It is a formula: not counted.
5. **Sum 1, his, on paper:** D4, Dublin at £90 (e.g. 38 × £90 = **£3,420**). The hint is the class method: "£90 is 9 × 10. First work out 38 × 9, then multiply by 10."
6. Finance fills in Paris and the plan (ƒ). E4 and F9 light green once, and the profit shows (e.g. £2,120 a day).

**WHY 27?** If a column carries fewer passengers than the people who want to fly at that fare, with seats to spare, a note explains it once. For example, at £110: "27 of the 30 can catch your flights … The other 3 want a time you don't fly."

**Counted: 1.**

**After the flights** (no Home, no Close the day):
1. **Launch Day: how it went**: the results, route by route, and a Next panel. Then **Check my plan for the week ▶**.
2. **The plan check**, "Your plan for the rest of week 1":
   - his flights with Launch Day's seats;
   - ✓ every flight fits in the day, ✓ every flight made money, ✓ the seats filled;
   - ● the people who couldn't get a seat, "a chance for later".

   A flight that lost money is an amber ! with **Fly it anyway**. **Change my plan** opens the Studio, and its sums work as before.

### Tuesday 3 to Saturday 7 Sep (days 2 to 6)

**Week 1 is flying**: a tile for each day, Tuesday to Sunday, filling in with each day's profit, then Live Operations and So far this week, with **Normal / Fast** and **Skip to Sunday ⏭**.

**Nothing to work out.** The Tuesday "Week 1 plan" sheet (the Paris tickets and the day's profit) is gone (hand-off 3). If he changed the plan at the check, the Changes tab and the sheet ask for what changed, as on any Monday.

### Weeks 1 to 2: P1, Sun 8 Sep (the week-1 accounts) and Mon 9 Sep

**Sunday: "How did week 1 go?"**
1. **Finance's example first**, with made-up numbers: it clicks Mon £1,250, Tue £1,300 and Wed £1,150, presses Enter, and the sheet shows **£3,700**.
2. **My turn ▶**: he clicks his own Mon to Sun profits into the formula bar (= B2+B3+…+B8) and presses **Enter**. The sheet adds them.
3. **Average a day** = B9 ÷ B10, built as a formula.
4. Two green done callouts, then **Plan week 2 ▶**.

**Monday, week 2 (hand-off 1c): a plain week.** The plan opener, then **the plan check** ("Your plan for week 2": last week's seats and profit, a day on average), then **"Week 2 is flying"**, Monday to Sunday. No Fuel desk, no what-if sheet, no shop, nothing new. If his plan is the one that flew in week 1, nothing is worked out: the cells Launch Day left to Finance (Paris's tickets, the day's profit) stay Finance's.

**Counted: none** (P1 has nothing scheduled). If he changes his plan on Monday, the Studio's sums for the change are counted, up to two.

### Weeks 2 to 3: P2, Sun 15 Sep and Mon 16 Sep

**Sunday: the week-2 accounts.** He clicks the days and adds them, as in week 1 (the ladder: click in weeks 1 and 2). The average, built as a formula. Not counted.

**Monday, week 3.**
- **The Fuel desk opens** (day 15): *Cash, tank, profit* plays at the first visit, then he chooses fuel blocks.
- **The what-if fare sheet opens** (the **What if** tab in the Studio, and a row in the opener's This week list).
- **Finance's questions, "Before week 3 flies"** (after his plan's own sums, before the Fuel desk). Two counted, on paper:
  1. **What-if: the first sum.** e.g. *What if Dublin were £100?* Seats filled a day 38 (ƒ) × £100 = **£3,800**.
  2. **The modelling question.** His plan leaves money on the table (another fare earns at least £50 a day more on his own flights), so: *Dublin: £90 or £100?* A small sheet with Now and What if columns: passengers, ticket revenue, costs and profit a flight (ƒ). He works out the **difference for one flight**: e.g. £627 − £437 = **+£190**; Finance shows a day (× 2 flights, £380) and a week (£2,660). If his plan is already good, Finance suggests two changes, one step each, and he picks one to work out (it may lose money).
- Then the plan check and **"Week 3 is flying"**.

**Concepts.** Passengers × fare; the difference between two models; scaling one flight to a day and a week.

### Weeks 3 to 4: P3, Sun 22 Sep and Mon 23 Sep

**Sunday: the week-3 accounts.** Click and add, then he is shown **Σ SUM** and drags it. The average as a formula. *Weekdays and weekends* plays, and the weekend-schedules request comes at this review.

**Monday, week 4.**
- **Four routes open:** Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Geneva, Barcelona. **The plane shop opens** (Saab 340 and ATR 72 in it). Stars go public (*Your star rating is public*).
- In the Studio, **View trade-offs** and **ƒ work it out** come back, and the **capacity tip** ("every seat is sold… another plane") can now show.
- If he buys a plane, *Two planes, one route* and *Can I afford the plane and still run the airline?* play, and **Buy now or wait?** asks the time to afford (counted). A new route's tickets are counted too. These are his plan's own sums: they take the pause's slots first.
- **Finance's questions**, with whatever room is left:
  1. **Time to afford** (unless the shop asked it). e.g. TEST Air can already buy everything in the shop (£56,210), so it is asked about the **E190**, on sale from day 29: (£70,000 − £56,210) ÷ £2,442 a day = 5.6, so **6 days**.
  2. **The first rotating question.** e.g. *Which day made the most profit?*, or, when two days tie, *the range*: best day − worst day.
- Then the plan check and **"Week 4 is flying"**.

**Concepts.** Division and rounding up; reading a table; the range.

### Weeks 4 to 5: P4, Sun 29 Sep and Mon 30 Sep

**Sunday: the week-4 accounts.** Σ SUM by dragging only, from now on. The average as a formula.

**Monday, week 5.**
- **Madrid opens** (*New today · Medium haul · Madrid: range*), and the E175 and E190 join the shop.
- **Finance's questions:**
  1. **Fuel for two planes**: litres a seat for his second type of plane (fuel a trip ÷ seats, one decimal place), beside the first type's. Asked when he has two types of plane; otherwise a rotating question takes its place.
  2. **Madrid's possible revenue**: who wants to fly a day × fare, e.g. 90 × £170 = **£15,300**.
- Week 5 keeps the board of the old week loop (the flying page is for weeks 1 to 4).

### Weeks 5 to 6: P5, Sun 6 Oct and Mon 7 Oct

**Sunday: "How did week 5 go?"** The total and the average, built. *Approve your regular plan*.

**Monday, week 6: the game now runs a week at a time** (day 36). Marrakech opens. There is no second review of Sunday: week 5's accounts did it.
- **Finance's questions:**
  1. **The difference from the plan, the first time:** e.g. projected £17,892, made £17,328: **£564** short of the plan.
  2. **A rotating question**, e.g. *What share of seats were filled in week 5?* (passengers ÷ seats × 100).

### Weeks 6 to 13 (October to the end of November)

A week at a time; each Monday opens with last week's review (built, not counted), then the plan.

**Finance's questions every Monday** (PauseSchedule P6 on): the **difference from the plan about every third pause**, otherwise a **rotating question** (best day, range, seats filled, worst day in turn), then the **modelling question**. At most two, after his plan's own sums: a pupil who changes his plan every week is asked his plan's sums and Finance's wait; one who keeps his plan gets Finance's two.

At the start of each month there is a **Finance audit**: Finance re-checks one line of the month's accounts, passengers × fare (counted). If the plan hasn't changed for weeks and a lot of profit was missed, it becomes a **board review**.

### December (a month at a time) and the chapter-one review, Sat 28 Dec (day 118)

December runs as one month: the audit and the plan.

**The chapter-one review: "Chapter 1: the accounts."** The months are in a table and he builds each line:
1. **Total revenue**: add the four months.
2. **Total costs.**
3. **Profit**: revenue − costs.
4. **Planned against what happened.**

*A year of demand* plays, and the seasonal plans upgrade is offered.

Then the card: **"End of chapter one. This test build stops here."**

## The maths across the chapter

| Idea | Where he meets it |
| --- | --- |
| Multiplication (passengers × fare) | Launch Day: Finance's formula, his formula (C4), Sum 1 on paper (D4, by the class method); a new route's tickets; the audit; the what-if sheet (free) |
| Comparing products (fuller ≠ better) | The three Dublin fares side by side on the Launch Day sheet; the fare briefing's slider; the what-if sheet (One flight, two ways was removed in hand-off 3) |
| Subtraction, including negative answers | Changes; the difference from plan; range |
| Adding a column (total) | Every week's accounts: click (week 1), Σ SUM shown (week 2), drag (week 3 on) |
| Mean (average a day) | Every week's accounts (a formula) |
| Division and rounding up | Time to afford |
| Rates (litres per seat) | P3, fuel for two planes (not built yet) |
| Reading a maximum or minimum | Best day, worst day |
| Time: durations and fitting a day | The timetable: "3 h 45 there and back", turnarounds, the day 06:00 to 22:00; the plan check's "every flight fits in the day" |
| Distance against range | The Madrid range check |
| Forecast against actual | The difference from plan; Projected on Monday |

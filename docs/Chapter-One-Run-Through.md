# Chapter one: a run-through

*For Bertie, 30 Oct 2026; Launch Day and week 1 brought up to date with hand-off 3 the same evening. What a pupil meets from Launch Day to the chapter-one review in the current build (workbook v6.2 and the 30 Oct hand-offs, then v6.3 and hand-off 3): the pauses, the maths at each one, and the questions he could be asked. Figures marked "e.g." come from a test run (the toolkit's TEST Air); his own will differ.*

## The shape of the chapter

- **Dates.** Launch Day is **Mon 2 Sep 2030 (day 1)**. The **chapter-one review is Sat 28 Dec 2030 (day 118)**, and the test build stops there with one card.
- **How time runs.**
  - Week 1: Launch Day, then a quick check of the plan, and Tuesday to Sunday fly on their own (hand-off 3).
  - Weeks 2 to 4: a week at a time, with a **Sunday review** and a **Monday plan**.
  - From week 5 (day 29): a week at a time, with the review opening the Monday.
  - From December: a month at a time.
- **A pause** is the review and the next plan together. At each pause he is asked **at most two counted questions**: a figure he works out in his book and types in. Anything else on the screen is worked out by Finance (ƒ), or he builds it as a formula on the sheet, which doesn't count. Nothing is asked between pauses.
- **Every question shows a Question Signal:**
  - **cyan "Build it on the sheet"**: click the cells and press Enter; doesn't count;
  - **gold "Work it out on paper"**: work it in his book, write the Sum number beside it, type the answer and press Enter; counts.
- **Parked for this build:** all one-off events and news, the Red Kite undercut, snacks and cabin sales, flight-time sums and the Flight times page, and the briefings Customers have a choice, One big Saturday, How long will it take? and Snacks on board.

## Week by week

### Launch Day: Mon 2 Sep (day 1)

**What happens.**
- A TEST airline skips the naming and painting; a pupil's own airline names it, paints the Twin Otter and takes the launch deal.
- The Studio opens on the **full-width timetable** with Dublin and Paris in the Add a flight tray (no Choose where to fly page). He drags flights onto the plane and sets the fares.
- Then **Work out my plan ▶** opens the **Launch Day sheet**, "Ticket revenue for each flight, at each fare". The column heads say whose flights they count: "your 2 flights" over "Dublin at £80".

**Briefings.**
- *What a fare does*: the first time he sets a fare. Beat 4 is one panel: the planes fill and empty as he moves the slider from £70 to £140.
- *How an airline makes money*: in the plan, but **not built yet**.

**Concepts.**
- Ticket revenue = passengers × fare, and a formula that does it: = B2 × B3.
- A fuller plane does not always earn more.
- Profit = revenue − costs.
- Fuel is a cost: 600 L at £1.20 = £720, in Total costs.

**The sheet's order (hand-off 3): left to right, the job first.** With his fare at £80 the three Dublin columns are £70, £80 and £90.
1. **The job first:** "Work out the ticket revenue for each flight, at each fare", and **Watch ▶**.
2. **Finance builds B4:** a pointer clicks B2, presses ×, clicks B3 and presses Enter (e.g. 38 × £70 = £2,660).
3. **HOW A FORMULA WORKS**: "= B2 × B3 means passengers × fare". B2 is cyan and B3 violet. Then **Your turn ▶**.
4. **He builds C4** with the keys or the keyboard and presses Enter himself (e.g. £3,040). It is a formula: not counted.
5. **Sum 1, his, on paper:** D4, Dublin at £90 (e.g. 38 × £90 = **£3,420**). The hint is the class method: "£90 is 9 × 10. First work out 38 × 9, then multiply by 10."
6. Finance fills in Paris and the plan (ƒ). E4 and F9 light green once, and the profit shows (e.g. £2,120 a day).

**WHY 27?** If a column carries fewer passengers than the people who want to fly at that fare, with seats to spare, a note explains it once. For example, at £110: "27 of the 30 can catch your flights … The other 3 want a time you don't fly."

**Counted: 1.**

**After the flights** (no Home, no Close the day):
1. **Launch Day: how it went**: the results, route by route, and a Next panel. Then **Check my plan for the week ▶**.
2. **The plan check**, "Your plan for the rest of week 1":
   - his flights with Launch Day's seats;
   - ✓ every flight fits in the day, ✓ every flight made money, ✓ the seats filled;
   - ● the people who couldn't get a seat, "a chance for later".

   A flight that lost money is an amber ! with **Fly it anyway**. **Change my plan** opens the Studio, and its sums work as before.

### Tuesday 3 to Saturday 7 Sep (days 2 to 6)

**Week 1 is flying**: a tile for each day, Tuesday to Sunday, filling in with each day's profit, then Live Operations and So far this week, with **Normal / Fast** and **Skip to Sunday ⏭**.

**Nothing to work out.** The Tuesday "Week 1 plan" sheet (the Paris tickets and the day's profit) is gone (hand-off 3). If he changed the plan at the check, the Changes tab and the sheet ask for what changed, as on any Monday.

### Weeks 1 to 2: P1, Sun 8 Sep (the week-1 accounts) and Mon 9 Sep

**Sunday: "How did week 1 go?"**
1. **Finance's example first**, with made-up numbers: it clicks Mon £1,250, Tue £1,300 and Wed £1,150, presses Enter, and the sheet shows **£3,700**.
2. **My turn ▶**: he clicks his own Mon to Sun profits into the formula bar (= B2+B3+…+B8) and presses **Enter**. The sheet adds them, e.g. £18,635.
3. **Average a day** = B9 ÷ B10 (e.g. £18,635 ÷ 7 = £2,662), built as a formula.
4. Two green done callouts, then **Plan week 2 ▶**.

**Concepts.** Adding a column (a total), the mean, and a formula as cell names.

**Counted: none** (P1 is open: Fable's what-if and modelling questions were moved off it).

**Monday, week 2.**
- The plan opener shows "This week", and the **what-if fare sheet** opens. He can try any fare with − / +, see passengers × fare and the change in profit, and use **Back to my fares** or **Set my fares**. It is free to try, with no sums.
- **The Fuel desk opens** (day 8). *Cash, tank, profit* plays first. Then he chooses fuel blocks. The order shows the cost (ƒ) and the cash after it against his reserve; nothing is bought until Continue.

**Concepts.**
- Price and demand: a higher fare means fewer passengers.
- Buying ahead: blocks of litres × price, and a reserve.

### Weeks 2 to 3: P2, Sun 15 Sep and Mon 16 Sep

**Sunday: the week-2 accounts.**
- He clicks the days and adds them. Then "There's a quicker way" shows **Σ SUM**: drag down the Profit column and press Enter. Then **Try it**.
- The average, built as a formula.
- **The first rotating question (counted):** e.g. **Best day**. Which day made the most, and how much? He reads the largest, or works it on paper.

The week-2 review ends with *Weekdays and weekends* and the Operations Request: "change weekdays and weekends separately" (the Days setting from week 3).

**Monday, week 3.**
- **Four routes open:** Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Geneva, Barcelona. Fares now step by £5.
- **The shop opens: a second Twin Otter.** *Two planes, one route* plays at his first comparison. *Can I afford the plane and still run the airline?* plays before his first purchase.
- **Time to afford (counted, P2 question 1):** (price − cash) ÷ profit a day, rounded up to whole days. It is in "Buy now or wait?".
- **Changes tab:** "Is the change worth it?". If he changed his plan: profit a day with the change − profit now. It is counted while the pause has room, otherwise built as a formula.
- Stars start to move from this week.

**Concepts.** Division, rounding up, and the difference between two figures (including a negative difference).

### Weeks 3 to 4: P3, Sun 22 Sep and Mon 23 Sep

**Sunday:** the accounts with Σ SUM (drag only from now) and the average (formulas).

**Monday:**
- **Madrid opens**, and two jets (E175, E190) are in the shop.
- *Madrid: range*: which aircraft can reach it? This compares the route's distance with each plane's range.

**PauseSchedule's questions, not built yet:**
- **Fuel for two planes**: litres per seat, a rate.
- **Madrid possible revenue**: passengers × fare on the market sheet.

**As built:** only a Changes question, when he changes his plan.

### Weeks 4 to 5: P4, Sun 29 Sep and Mon 30 Sep

**Sunday:** the accounts. This week the sheet shows **Projected on Monday** and the **Difference from the plan**.

**Counted:**
1. **The difference from plan, for the first time:** actual − projected. E.g. £17,670 − £17,921 = **−£251**: below the plan.
2. **A rotating question**, e.g. **Range**: best day − worst day. E.g. £2,568 − £2,473 = **£95**.

**Concepts.** Subtraction with a negative answer, and comparing a forecast with what happened.

From next week the game runs a week at a time.

### Week 5: P5, Mon 7 Oct

The review opens the Monday ("How did week 5 go?"). The average a day is a Build here, not counted.

**PauseSchedule:** the **E175 worth check** if he's weighing a jet, otherwise a rotating question; then a **modelling question**. Neither is built yet.

**As built** (e.g.): a **new route's tickets** (e.g. Brussels: passengers × fare) and a **Changes** question.

### Weeks 6 to 13 (October to the end of November)

A week at a time; each review opens the Monday.

**PauseSchedule:** required decisions first (a plane, a new route), then the **difference about every third pause**, or a rotating or modelling question. At most two.

**As built:** most weeks ask **nothing counted** unless he changes his plan (Changes) or opens a route (its tickets). The report's section 5 proposes moving these reviews onto the sheet so the difference and the rotating questions can be asked.

At the start of each month there is a **Finance audit**: Finance re-checks one line of the month's accounts, passengers × fare (counted). If the plan hasn't changed for weeks and a lot of profit was missed, it becomes a **board review**.

### December (a month at a time) and the chapter-one review, Sat 28 Dec (day 118)

December runs as one month: the audit and the plan.

**The chapter-one review: "Chapter 1: the accounts."** The months are in a table and he builds each line:
1. **Total revenue**: add the four months.
2. **Total costs.**
3. **Profit**: revenue − costs.
4. **Planned against what happened.**

*A year of demand* plays, and the seasonal plans upgrade is offered.

Then the card: **"End of chapter one. This test build stops here."**

## The maths across the chapter

| Idea | Where he meets it |
| --- | --- |
| Multiplication (passengers × fare) | Launch Day: Finance's formula, his formula (C4), Sum 1 on paper (D4, by the class method); a new route's tickets; the audit; the what-if sheet (free) |
| Comparing products (fuller ≠ better) | The three Dublin fares side by side on the Launch Day sheet; the fare briefing's slider; the what-if sheet (One flight, two ways was removed in hand-off 3) |
| Subtraction, including negative answers | Changes; the difference from plan; range |
| Adding a column (total) | Every week's accounts: click (week 1), Σ SUM shown (week 2), drag (week 3 on) |
| Mean (average a day) | Every week's accounts (a formula) |
| Division and rounding up | Time to afford |
| Rates (litres per seat) | P3, fuel for two planes (not built yet) |
| Reading a maximum or minimum | Best day, worst day |
| Time: durations and fitting a day | The timetable: "3 h 45 there and back", turnarounds, the day 06:00 to 22:00; the plan check's "every flight fits in the day" |
| Distance against range | The Madrid range check |
| Forecast against actual | The difference from plan; Projected on Monday |

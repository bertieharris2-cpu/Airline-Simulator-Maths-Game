# Balance Report — v6.0 (19 Oct 2026)
*Travels with Airline-World-Workbook-v6.0.xlsx. Checks D104: a richer first eight weeks.*

## What was tested
Five eight-week plans on Paris and Dublin with the Twin Otter, at the chapter-1 fuel prices (v5.17, with the week-4 and week-5 dips), under the new rules: growth that responds to how a route is served, stars from week 3, the one-flight adaptation rule (D101). The reference engine is the Pass-1 model (a service is a round trip; time-locked and flexible passengers; costs per service and per day).

| Plan | 8-week profit | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C · £90 both routes, mixed day, second Twin Otter in week 4 | £110,600 | the standard good plan |
| B2 · £100 both routes, second Twin Otter in week 4 | £108,600 | a different good plan: fewer passengers, more each; stars suffer from people turned away |

## What this shows
1. **There are already two good plans in the first eight weeks** — the £90 plan and the £100 plan with a second plane finish within 2% of each other — and the gap to the bad plans is wide. That is the shape Bertie asked for.
2. **In the first eight weeks the plane is the limit, not demand.** With one or two Twin Otters every plan is capacity-bound by week 3, so the new growth rules change almost nothing before week 9: they are a chapter-length effect. By December a "cheap and build" airline has Dublin at about 1.6× its opening demand and fills an ATR; a "top fare" airline's Dublin has not grown. That is the long-game trade-off, and it is honest, but it should not be sold as variety in weeks 1–8.
3. **The variety in weeks 1–8 therefore has to come from capacity and route choices and from the curveballs** — which is what the six new events do: each has two or three defensible answers (take the school booking or hold the fare; fly the strike with delays or cancel; the tanker at £2.40 against a falling forecast; the slot squeeze that breaks the morning peak). Together with the week-3 route choice (Amsterdam against Frankfurt, Geneva, Barcelona) and the second-plane timing, a pupil's first eight weeks can differ in at least four decisions from another pupil's, and both can be right.
4. **Track B** gives a second autumn for pupils who have played: the storm in week 4, the fault in week 3, the crew sickness in week 7, the price war in week 8; a Dublin concert instead of the rugby; an Amsterdam fair instead of Milan's. Same rules, different order; the curveballs that teach something play on both.

## Parameters set
Archetypes: base growth 1 / 1 / 3 / 2 / 4 % a week (business, city, leisure, sun, long haul); +2 / +2 / +3 / +3 / +2 when the week's fill was ≥ 80% and people were turned away; no growth at the top fare, half at the second from top; −3 / −3 / −4 / −4 / −2 a week while not flown; ceilings 1.5 / 1.6 / 1.8 / 1.8 / 1.6.

## Knock-ons to re-run later
The raised ceilings lift chapter 2–4 demand on routes flown well from the start; the chapter-2 re-run should check that long-haul planes do not fill too easily (reduce the long-haul ceiling to 1.4 if they do). Stars from week 3 make the "turned away" penalty bite in chapter 1; the month review should show the pupil why a half-star went, so it reads as information.
